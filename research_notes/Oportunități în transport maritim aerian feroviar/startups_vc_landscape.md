## 1. Freight/logistics tech VC funding trends 2021–2026 (totals, by mode, by region)

### Takeaway
Supply-chain/logistics venture funding fell from more than $21B in 2021 to about $11B in 2022, then to about $9B in 2025. 2026 is rebounding: $8.4B by 12 Aug 2026, on pace for the best year since 2022, driven by AI and "physical AI". But logistics' share of total VC is shrinking (about 2.0% of 2025 global VC, about 1.2% of H1 2026), because general AI rounds are growing much faster. Maritime-software funding in the narrow sense is small and fell in 2026 (Tracxn: $85.2M YTD, down about 35% vs 2025). Maritime money is concentrated in a few large defense- and sensing-adjacent rounds.

### Inferences
- **Shape of the cycle:** peak in 2021 (more than $21B), roughly halved in 2022 (about $11B), trough in 2023–2025 (2025 about $9B), recovery in 2026. A linear extrapolation of $8.4B by day 224 gives about **$13–14B for 2026**. That is roughly 60–65% of the 2021 peak. The figure is the researcher's arithmetic, and deal flow is lumpy.
- **Share is shrinking even as dollars recover.** Logistics was about 2.0% of global VC in 2025 ($9B of $440B) and about 1.2% in H1 2026 ($6.2B of $510B). Generalist AI money is not flowing to logistics in proportion. Founders need either an "AI-native" story or a "physical AI/defense" angle to tap the larger pools.
- **Average H1 2026 round was about $17.7M** ($6.2B / 350). Capital is concentrated in fewer, larger rounds. PitchBook said Q2 2025 deal *counts* were at an 8-year low even as values held up.
- **Maritime splits in two.** Classic shipping SaaS (chartering, fleet management, voyage tools) is a small, shrinking VC category (Tracxn's $85M). Sensing/"maritime domain awareness", video AI and safety (Quartermaster, ShipIn) attract large US rounds from generalist and security-oriented investors. A Romanian founder pitching "maritime SaaS" will face a thin investor pool. "Maritime data/AI with a security, insurance or compliance buyer" looks better funded.
- **PE is buying freight *software*, not carriers.** That points to a viable exit path (PE roll-up or strategic buyer) for profitable, capital-efficient vertical SaaS even without IPO-scale growth.
- PitchBook's Q1 2026 figure ($3.3B, +74% YoY) implies a Q1 2025 base of about $1.9B. That conflicts with PitchBook's own earlier Q1 2025 "supply chain tech" figure of $2.4B, probably because of different scope (logistics vs supply chain tech, Waymo exclusion). Cite each figure with its own scope.

## 2. Notable successes, exits and scale-ups: what made them work

### Takeaway
The winners in this sector are mostly **data or software consolidators that own a hard-to-replicate dataset or a system of record**: Kpler (rolled up MarineTraffic, FleetMon and Spire Maritime), WiseTech (CargoWise, plus the $2.1B e2open deal) and Trimble–Transporeon. The other route is **capital-intensive deep tech with a regulated pilot path** (Parallel Systems, Einride via SPAC). Asset-light "digital forwarders" (Flexport, Forto) reached large valuations but then struggled with services economics.

### Inferences
- **What worked, pattern 1: own the data layer, then consolidate.** Kpler built a commodity/cargo intelligence product, then bought AIS networks (terrestrial with MarineTraffic and FleetMon, satellite with Spire). That creates a moat no single startup can copy. A data/ML founder can use the same play in a niche: become the best dataset plus model for one decision (for example port-call ETAs, air-cargo capacity, rail wagon health) and be the obvious acquisition target.
- **Pattern 2: system of record plus M&A.** WiseTech and Descartes grow by buying small vertical tools and folding them into a core platform. For a startup this is both the most likely exit and a threat, because incumbents bundle AI features fast. WiseTech's own "AI program" is cited in its FY26 results.
- **Pattern 3: integrate with the incumbent system of record instead of replacing it** (ZeroNorth on Veson IMOS; cargo.one adding ocean via Cargofive). This shortens sales cycles.
- **Pattern 4: strategic investors who are also buyers.** Tokio Marine in ShipIn and Maersk Growth in Einride/Fleetzero signal that insurers and carriers fund tools that cut their own losses or emissions.
- **Deep tech rail and autonomy take 5+ years and $200M+.** Parallel Systems was founded around 2020 and is still in an FRA-supervised pilot in 2026. That path is unsuitable for a solo technical founder without deep-tech VC access.
- Valuation reset: Einride's SPAC valuation fell from $1.8B announced to about $1.35B reported. De-SPAC valuations in mobility keep compressing.

## 3. Notable failures and down rounds: why they failed, and common anti-patterns

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are: (1) **asset-light intermediation run on venture money** (digital brokerage and forwarding: Convoy, Forto, Flexport's repeated layoffs), whose thin take rates cannot carry SaaS-style burn through a freight down-cycle; (2) **industry-utility platforms controlled by one incumbent** (TradeLens), which competitors won't join; (3) **hype technologies without a near-term buyer** (blockchain trade platforms, hyperloop, full autonomy at sea); (4) **post-SPAC micro-caps** (Freightos) that are still unprofitable years after listing.

- Q2 2026 revenue was a **record $7.7M (+3% YoY)**. **Platform revenue was $2.9M (+19%)**, driven by WebCargo and customs, while **SaaS ("Solutions") was weaker than expected**. - Thetius (about 2023) counted only **about $113M of VC into autonomous/unmanned surface vessel startups since 2010**. That is tiny next to the hype. — [Thetius](https://thetius.com/maritime-autonomous-surface-ship-market-map/)

### Inferences
- **Marketplace take rates are brutally thin.** Freightos' platform revenue of $2.9M on $422M GBV is about a **0.7% take rate**, and total revenue / GBV is about 1.8%. Even with GBV up 33%, the company is not yet profitable at about $31M revenue. Pure transactional air/ocean marketplaces need very large volume or adjacent higher-margin services (customs, payments, financing) to work.
- **Digital forwarders are services businesses with software valuations.** Flexport traded at 2.5x revenue at the peak, and its revenue is mostly pass-through freight. Forto burned more than $600M and cut sales staff. Both moved toward selling software and AI to other forwarders. **Anti-pattern: competing with your customers** (becoming a forwarder) and then trying to sell them software.
- **Freight-cycle exposure:** companies that depend on transaction volume or spot rates (brokerage, forwarding, marketplaces) suffered in the 2023–2024 freight recession. Subscription SaaS sold to carriers, ports and shippers was more resilient (PE "embraces freight tech").
- **Founder and governance risk matters at scale.** WiseTech's founder-governance crisis and Freightos' founder board exit both moved share prices sharply.
- **Common anti-patterns (synthesis):** (a) raising growth equity to subsidize low-margin volume; (b) building "neutral" industry platforms owned by one carrier; (c) technology-first offerings (blockchain, hyperloop, full autonomy) with no regulatory path or paying buyer; (d) SPAC listings at sub-scale revenue; (e) pilots that never convert (see section 6).

## 6. Business-model patterns, ACVs, sales cycles, public procurement and pilot conversion

### Takeaway
Data subscriptions and system-of-record SaaS, sold mostly to private operators and shippers, show the most durable economics in this sector. Marketplaces and forwarding run at roughly 1–2% of booking value. Hardware-plus-software fleet deployments (ShipIn) scale per vessel. No hard, sourced ACV or sales-cycle benchmarks for ports, airlines or railways were found this session. That is a key gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Transactional marketplace economics:** Freightos had Q2 2026 platform revenue of $2.9M on $422M GBV, total revenue of $7.7M, and was still loss-making. Its SaaS segment underperformed while transactions grew. — [Freightos Q2 2026](https://www.freightos.com/press-release/freightos-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/)
- **Services-wrapped software (forwarding) economics:** Flexport's $8B valuation was about 2.5x revenue (2022) — [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/flexport). It reached about $2.1B revenue in 2024 but was not organically profitable through 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/flexport/). Forto burned more than $600M and pivoted to AI software — [Teardown](https://www.teardown.ai/companies/forto).
- **Software consolidator scale:** WiseTech FY26 revenue was US$1.40B, including US$541.2M from e2open over 11 months. — [Kalkine](https://kalkine.com.au/news/technology/wisetech-global-asxwtc-fy26-revenue-reaches-us140-billion-as-e2open-integration-and-ai-program-reshape-the-business)
- **Data-subscription consolidation:** Kpler paid about $241M for Spire Maritime's satellite-AIS business. — [Virginia Business](https://virginiabusiness.com/spire-sells-maritime-business-for-241m-to-kpler/)
- **Per-vessel fleet deployment:** ShipIn has 92 shipowners and more than 1,300 vessels, an average of about 14 vessels per customer. — [Smart Maritime Network](https://smartmaritimenetwork.com/2026/08/04/shipin-raises-52m-to-expand-ai-platform/)
- **Integration-led distribution:** ZeroNorth plugged into Veson IMOS by API, with about 300 vessels committed at launch. — [ZeroNorth](https://zeronorth.com/press/veson-zeronorth-partnership)
- **Regulated rail pilot path:** Parallel Systems runs its pilot with a short-line railroad (Genesee & Wyoming) under FRA oversight, about six years after founding. — [TMCnet](https://www.tmcnet.com/usubmit/2026/10/07/10459143.htm)
- **Corporate pilots via accelerators:** the ZEBOX UK cohort is 10 startups. The CMA CGM Startup Awards pick 60 finalists. — [Startups Magazine](https://startupsmagazine.co.uk/article-zebox-global-startup-accelerator-opens-manchester); [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/cma-cgm-group-launches-the-third-edition-of-the-cma-cgm-startup-awards-at-vivatech/)

### Inferences
- **Ranking of models for a data/ML founder** (researcher's synthesis from the cases above):
  1. **Data subscription / intelligence**. Kpler and Xeneta show it can work. The moat is a proprietary dataset, and the exit is consolidation.
  2. **Workflow SaaS integrated into an existing system of record** (CargoWise, Veson IMOS, port community systems). Faster sales, and acquirers are obvious.
  3. **Compliance-as-a-service**, where regulation forces purchase on a deadline (emissions reporting, customs pre-arrival data, e-documents). This creates urgency that ordinary productivity tools lack.
  4. **AI "digital worker" pricing** (per shipment, per document, per seat-equivalent). Now crowded (section 7).
  5. **Marketplaces / take rate**. Avoid unless liquidity is already there: about 0.7% platform take rate at Freightos.
  6. **Becoming a forwarder or broker.** Avoid. Cyclical, capital-hungry, and it puts you in competition with your customers.
- **Buyer segments by ease of selling** (synthesis): mid-size forwarders and NVOCCs, plus ship managers and owners with 10–100 vessels, have short decision chains. ShipIn's average of about 14 vessels per customer shows mid-size fleets do buy. Top-5 carriers and integrators buy slowly and often build in-house. **Port authorities and state railways** (CFR in Romania, DB, SNCF, PKP) are subject to public-procurement law, so expect tenders, long cycles and price-driven awards. Better entry points are private terminal operators, rail freight operators, wagon lessors and short-lines.
- **Pilot-to-production conversion** is the core risk with corporates. Good mitigations are paid pilots with pre-agreed success metrics and conversion pricing, and insurer or operator co-investment, as with Tokio Marine in ShipIn.

### Gaps
- **No sourced ACV or sales-cycle benchmarks** were found for selling to ports, carriers, airlines, railways, forwarders or shippers. No pilot-to-production conversion rates were found either. Search budget was exhausted. Suggested sources: Thetius buyer surveys, Lufthansa Innovation Hub venture-clienting reports, founder interviews on The Loadstar and FreightWaves, Bessemer/Insight vertical-SaaS benchmarks.
- **Background, not verified this session:** EU public procurement by ports and railway operators generally falls under the **Utilities Directive 2014/25/EU** (transport-sector entities), with EU-wide tender obligations above value thresholds of a few hundred thousand euros for services. In practice, public port and rail authorities often run 6–18+ month cycles. Treat these as rules of thumb to verify, not sourced figures. Industry rules of thumb often quoted: mid-market forwarder SaaS ACV in the low tens of thousands of dollars, and enterprise carrier or shipper deals in the six figures with 6–12 month cycles. These are unverified.

### Inferences
White spaces ranked for a data/ML founder in Romania targeting the EU and US. This is the researcher's synthesis.
1. **European rail freight data and operations AI.** Wagon and asset health (Konux and Nexxiot exist, but the market is fragmented), path/slot and capacity prediction, cross-border consignment-note and handover automation, and predictive ETAs for intermodal (rail-road-port). Rail freight is policy-favoured in the EU, with a modal-shift agenda, yet VC-thin (no large 2026 EU rail-software rounds surfaced). Buyers: private rail freight operators, wagon lessors, intermodal terminals. Avoid state incumbents as a first customer. Romania and CEE (Constanța–Danube–rail corridors, Ukraine-related rerouting) offer a home-market test bed. Assumption to validate.
2. **Maritime compliance and emissions data products** (EU ETS for shipping, FuelEU Maritime, verifiable fuel and emissions data for charter-party clauses). Regulation-forced demand, a data/ML-heavy problem, and buyers among mid-size owners, managers and charterers. Incumbents (ZeroNorth, Veson, Kpler) are present, so differentiate on verification, audit and claims workflows.
3. **Maritime risk AI for insurers and P&I** (casualty and claims prediction, sanctions and dark-fleet risk from AIS plus satellite data). This is validated by the Tokio Marine–ShipIn and Quartermaster rounds and by Windward's existence. Insurers are a faster, richer buyer than shipowners.
4. **Air-cargo intelligence and agents**: capacity forecasting, dynamic pricing for airlines and GSAs, ULD and handling optimization, AWB/eAWB and ICS2 data-quality agents. Booking-layer players exist (cargo.one, Freightos/WebCargo), but airline revenue management for cargo is still largely legacy.
5. **Mode-specific AI agents with system-of-record write-back** (see section 7). Only defensible with deep CargoWise or SAP integration plus proprietary performance data.
6. **Port and terminal optimization for mid-size and Black Sea/Mediterranean ports** (berth planning, gate and truck appointment, yard ML). Public port authorities are slow buyers, so sell to private terminal operators instead.
- **Areas to avoid as a first venture:** digital forwarding or brokerage, open marketplaces, blockchain trade platforms, capital-intensive autonomy (ships, trains), and horizontal "AI inbox for forwarders" without a wedge.
