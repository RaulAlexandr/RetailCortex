## 1. eFTI Regulation (EU) 2020/1056: acts, certification, acceptance date, scope

### Takeaway
The "mid-2026" acceptance date in the brief is out of date. Sources consistently put full application at **9 July 2027**: from then, Member State authorities must accept regulatory freight information made available electronically by operators through **certified eFTI platforms**. The obligation falls on authorities. Operators stay free to keep using paper. The certification layer (rules for platform and service-provider certification, plus conformity-assessment bodies) has been the slowest part and was reportedly still being finalised in 2026. That makes "certified eFTI platform / eFTI service provider" a greenfield market taking shape over 2026-2027.

### Cited Findings
- From **9 July 2027**, the eFTI Regulation applies in full, and Member State authorities must accept information shared electronically by operators via certified eFTI platforms. — [Evrotrust](https://evrotrust.com/the-efti-regulation-explained-how-qualified-trust-services-prepare-your-freight-business-for-july-2027/); [formrift](https://www.formrift.com/blog/eu-efti-2027-electronic-freight-information); [planlogi](https://planlogi.com/eng/ecmr-efti-2027-guide); [ybil.io](https://ybil.io/efti-2027); [EC DG MOVE eFTI page](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/logistics-and-multimodal-transport/efti-regulation_en) (page not fetchable from this session)
- The first set of eFTI implementing and delegated acts entered into force in **January 2025**. — [eFTI4EU news](https://efti4eu.eu/news/efti-implementing-delegated-acts-entered-into-force/); [eFTI4ALL](https://efti4all.eu/eu-acts-enforced-efti-progresses/)
- The Commission planned to adopt the remaining specifications, covering eFTI platform functional and technical requirements and certification rules for platforms and service providers, by about **December 2025**. A 2026 source says the certification and technical-specification acts were "still awaited" and that the schedule had slipped by nearly two years. — [Seeburger blog](https://blog.seeburger.com/efti-in-logistics-whats-changing-under-the-eu-regulation-and-how-companies-can-benefit/); [Tachoscan Control](https://tachoscancontrol.com/en/aktualnosci/the-revolution-of-electronic-shipping-documents-implementation-of-efti-in-the-eu-and-poland-in-the-light-of-legal-changes/) (search snippets; the exact attribution of the "slipped by nearly two years" claim among these could not be confirmed)
- The eFTI4EU Member State consortium's March 2026 timeline pairs a **delegated act on certification rules for eFTI platforms** with an **implementing act on platform requirements**, both tied to the 2027 application date. It shows **platform operations starting in January 2026**, from when platforms and providers can prepare and authorities may voluntarily start accepting data. — [eFTI4EU general presentation, 5 March 2026](https://efti4eu.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/eFTI4EU-General-Presentation_official_5-March-2026.pdf); [Seeburger](https://blog.seeburger.com/efti-in-logistics-whats-changing-under-the-eu-regulation-and-how-companies-can-benefit/)
- Only platforms that have passed **conformity assessment / certification** qualify under eFTI. — [Evrotrust](https://evrotrust.com/the-efti-regulation-explained-how-qualified-trust-services-prepare-your-freight-business-for-july-2027/)
- eFTI does **not ban paper**. It obliges authorities to accept digital information, so operator take-up is voluntary. — [Kaira Digital](https://kairadigital.com/en/blog/efti-what-is-it/); [European Shippers' Council](https://europeanshippers.eu/policies/digitalisation/)
- eFTI is framed as the core of the EU's paperless multimodal freight agenda, with eFTI4EU and eFTI4ALL as Member State and industry pilots. — [ERTICO](https://www.ertico.com/efti-regulation-digital-transformation-of-eu-freight-transport); [ETP-ALICE](https://www.etp-logistics.eu/towards-paperless-freight-transport-system-in-the-eu/)
- **[background, not re-verified]** Scope: regulatory information required under EU acts for **road, rail, inland waterway and air** freight. This covers combined transport (Dir. 92/106), cabotage (Reg. 1072/2009), dangerous goods (Dir. 2008/68: ADR/RID/ADN), waste shipments, and rail/road freight-rate rules (Reg. 11/1960). Maritime is outside eFTI and handled by EMSWe. The architecture has three layers: (a) **certified eFTI platforms** run by or for operators, (b) **eFTI service providers** that operate platforms, and (c) national **eFTI Gates** through which control authorities query data. The first acts were Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1942 and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2024 (common data set, authority access procedures). Text: [EUR-Lex 32020R1056](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32020R1056)
- eFTI vendors visible in 2026 search results include Seeburger (EDI/integration), Evrotrust (qualified trust services and e-signatures for eFTI), Kaira Digital, Planlogi, Formrift and ybil.io, mostly e-CMR/TMS players positioning for 2027. — URLs above

### Inferences
- Demand comes from two sides. (1) Authorities need eFTI Gates; Member States build these, often via eFTI4EU, so the market is limited and procurement-led. (2) Operators (shippers, forwarders, carriers, rail and IWW operators) will want certified platforms or must ensure their TMS/e-CMR provider is certified. Since operators are not obliged, the near-term buyer is the **platform or service provider seeking certification**: TMS, e-CMR and visibility vendors. These firms need conformity-assessment-ready modules such as data-set mapping, eFTI Gate APIs, audit logging, identity/trust services and access control.
- The certification acts are late, but the 9 July 2027 date has not been reported as postponed. That leaves a compressed window (late 2026 to mid-2027) for vendors to get certified. A "certification-as-a-service / eFTI compliance SDK" for small TMS vendors and national carriers looks underserved.
- Multimodal angle: rail (CIM consignment note), IWW and air cargo documents all fall within scope. Most eFTI marketing targets road e-CMR, so rail, IWW and air eFTI connectors are a thinner market.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact Official Journal numbers and dates for the **certification delegated act** and the **platform-requirements implementing act**, or whether they were adopted in 2026. The EC eFTI page and EUR-Lex were unreachable from this session.
- I found no figures on accredited conformity assessment bodies, certified platforms to date, or eFTI Gate go-live status per Member State.
- Affected population is unverified. **[background]** The EU has roughly 500k-600k road freight enterprises (Eurostat order of magnitude) plus forwarders and rail/IWW operators, but I found no eFTI-specific estimate.
- It is unclear whether any 2026 "omnibus" or military-mobility proposal touches eFTI timing.

## 2. Customs: ICS2, EU Customs Reform and de minimis, UCC IT (NCTS/AES), CBAM, EUDR, Forced Labour Regulation

### Takeaway
Customs is the densest compliance-driven software market and is now mostly **"live"**. ICS2 Release 3 became mandatory for road and rail on 1 Sep 2025, with derogations to 31 Dec 2025. NCTS Phase 6 and the AES-NCTS link are rolling out in 2026. CBAM's definitive regime started 1 Jan 2026. Next come EUDR on **30 Dec 2026** for large and medium operators, the EU Customs Reform (finally adopted autumn 2026) with an **EU Customs Data Hub from 2028** for e-commerce, a **€2 per-item handling fee from 1 Nov 2026** on top of the €3 duty applied since 1 Jul 2026, and the Forced Labour Regulation on **14 Dec 2027**.

### Inferences
- ICS2, NCTS and AES demand peaked in 2024-2025 and is now **served by incumbents**: Descartes, AEB, MIC, Customs Support, Dakosy, broker in-house tools, plus integrators **[background]**. The remaining opportunities are (a) multiple-filing data orchestration between carriers, forwarders and marketplaces, (b) NCTS P6 + ICS2 combined transit/ENS for road hauliers in smaller markets, and (c) data-quality and validation tooling (HS-6, EORI, consignee detail).
- The **Customs Reform** opens a large new cycle. The Data Hub (2028 e-commerce, to 2034) shifts the model from declarations to continuous data supply, with "importer of record" duties for online platforms and Trust & Check status. Software that builds data pipelines from marketplaces, 3PLs and carriers into the Data Hub will be needed from **2027-2028**. A technical founder could start now, but specs will only firm up with implementing acts.
- **EUDR (30 Dec 2026)** is the nearest hard deadline with a large population. For logistics, the gap is linking due-diligence statement (DDS) reference numbers and geolocation to customs declarations and shipment records. Pure traceability vendors are many, but few are integrated with forwarders' customs flows.
- **CBAM**: the obligated population shrank after the 50 t simplification, while the 2027 money flows (certificate purchase, verified embedded-emissions data, first declaration 30 Sep 2027) create demand for supplier-data collection and verification workflows. The market is crowded with ESG and customs vendors.
- **Forced labour**: 2027 application plus a missing database means 2026-2027 is a pre-market phase. Demand will be for supplier mapping and evidence repositories that can answer an investigation. This overlaps with EUDR and CSDDD tooling.

### Gaps
- I found no official count of operators filing ICS2 R3 road/rail or NCTS P6.
- CBAM affected numbers are unverified. **[background, not re-verified]** The Commission's Omnibus simplification (Reg. (EU) 2025/2083) reportedly exempts about 90% of importers while keeping about 99% of embedded emissions in scope.
- The EU Customs Authority's seat and operational start date were not checked this session. **[background, unverified]** Lille was reportedly selected as the seat in 2025.
- The exact OJ reference for the UCC recast (2026/2108?) and the precise Data Hub dates in the final text need verification.
- I found no EUDR count of obligated operators/traders, and no count of DDS submitted to TRACES.

## 3. Maritime: EU ETS shipping, FuelEU Maritime, EMSWe, AFIR shore power, ports and maritime strategies

### Takeaway
Shipping has moved from preparation to annual compliance cycles. **EU ETS** requires 70% surrender for 2025 emissions (in 2026) and 100% for 2026 emissions (surrendered in 2027), and CH4 and N2O are covered from 2026. **FuelEU Maritime** completed its first cycle in 2026: reports by 31 Jan, verified by 31 Mar, pooling and banking by 30 Apr, penalties and FuelEU Document of Compliance by 30 Jun. **EMSWe** formally applies from **15 Aug 2025**, but national roll-out is unsynchronised, with up to ~18 months of lag (into early 2027). The **2030** shore-power obligations (AFIR for ports, FuelEU for ships) drive OPS planning, metering and berth-scheduling software. The new **EU Ports Strategy** and **Industrial Maritime Strategy** (both 4 Mar 2026) are policy, not law.

### Cited Findings
**EU ETS maritime**
- Surrender phase-in: 40% of 2024 emissions (surrendered 2025), **70% of 2025 emissions (2026)**, **100% from 2026 emissions (2027 onward)**. — [EC DG CLIMA FAQ maritime ETS](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/transport-decarbonisation/reducing-emissions-shipping-sector/faq-maritime-transport-eu-emissions-trading-system-ets_en)
- **Methane (CH4) and nitrous oxide (N2O)** are included in the ETS from **1 Jan 2026** and have been monitored under MRV since 2024. — [IIMS](https://www.iims.org.uk/new-eu-ets-rules-take-effect-from-january-2026/); [Naturvårdsverket](https://www.naturvardsverket.se/en/guidance/eu-emissions-trading-system-ets/maritime-transport-in-the-eu-emissions-trading-system-ets/)
- Offshore ships ≥400 GT have reported under MRV since 2025. **Offshore ships >5,000 GT enter the ETS from 1 Jan 2027**, with first surrender by 30 Sep 2028. The Commission must review inclusion of ships between **400 and 5,000 GT** by **31 Dec 2026**. No review outcome had been published in the sources found. — [EC FAQ](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/transport-decarbonisation/reducing-emissions-shipping-sector/faq-maritime-transport-eu-emissions-trading-system-ets_en); [IIMS](https://www.iims.org.uk/new-eu-ets-rules-take-effect-from-january-2026/)

**FuelEU Maritime (Regulation (EU) 2023/1805)**
- First reporting period **2025**, first verification period **2026**. The first FuelEU report went to the verifier by **31 Jan 2026**. Verified reports go into the FuelEU database (THETIS-MRV) by **31 March**. Pooling, banking and borrowing choices are due by **30 April 2026**. Penalty payment and the **FuelEU Document of Compliance** are due by **30 June 2026**, then annually. — [EC DG MOVE FuelEU page](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-modes/maritime/decarbonising-maritime-transport-fueleu-maritime_en); [DNV](https://www.dnv.com/news/2025/fueleu-maritime-flexibility-mechanisms-and-pool-verification/); [BDO](https://www.bdo.co.uk/en-gb/insights/industries/shipping-and-transport/fueleu-maritime-regulation-how-it-works). One source says the report goes via THETIS-MRV by 31 March, so the deadlines are inconsistent across sources.
- GHG-intensity reduction targets: **-2% (2025), -6% (2030), -14.5% (2035), -31% (2040), -62% (2045), -80% (2050)**. — [KR FuelEU Guidelines Rev.2](https://www.krs.co.kr/TECHNICAL_FILE/KR%20FuelEU%20Maritime%20Guidelines_Rev.2.pdf)
- Penalty: **€2,400 per tonne of VLSFO-energy-equivalent** of deficit. A ship joins only one pool per period and cannot pool and borrow in the same period. — [Ecofreight](https://ecofreight.co/blog/fueleu-maritime-pool-explained); [Varuna Sentinels](https://www.varuna-sentinels.com/what-is-fueleu-maritime); [Carboneer](https://carboneer.earth/en/2025/08/fueleu-maritime-compliance-2025/)

### Inferences
- **ETS + FuelEU** created a compliance-data market (MRV, verification, EUA procurement, FuelEU pooling, cost pass-through clauses) that is **well served** by class societies (DNV, LR, BV, KR), verifiers and specialised SaaS. Gaps remain in: (a) **FuelEU pool marketplaces and surplus trading** between owners (thin liquidity, no public pool data); (b) **charterer-owner ETS/FuelEU cost allocation and invoicing** (BIMCO clauses); (c) a possible **400-5,000 GT extension**, which would bring in thousands of smaller general-cargo and offshore operators with little existing tooling if the end-2026 review leads to a proposal. - **EMSWe** is a "lagging state" market. While national windows go live through 2026-early 2027, ship agents and operators calling at several EU ports need **one integration across ~22 coastal Member States' RIMs**. Port-call middleware and API aggregation is a practical niche, as are port-community-system vendors adapting to the EMSWe data set.
- **Shore power 2030** pushes ports toward OPS capacity planning, energy management, berth allocation linked to OPS availability, and FuelEU at-berth compliance evidence. Ports buy through public procurement and CEF/AFIF co-funding, so long sales cycles should be expected.

## 4. Aviation: ReFuelEU Aviation, EU ETS aviation (non-CO2 MRV), EES/ETIAS, Single European Sky

### Takeaway
Aviation compliance software demand centres on **SAF accounting** (ReFuelEU 2% blending through 2029, 6% in 2030; first full review of the 2025 reporting data in 2026; formal review report in 2027) and the **non-CO2 MRV** that has applied since 1 Jan 2025. That MRV could lead to a non-CO2 ETS multiplier or a legislative proposal after 2027. **EES** became fully operational on **10 Apr 2026**. **ETIAS** has slipped from "Q4 2026" and is now expected in **2027**. The obligated population is small (airlines, fuel suppliers, airports, border authorities) and served by specialists.

### Inferences
- SAF and non-CO2 MRV tools (fuel-uplift reconciliation, SAF certificates/book-and-claim, per-flight non-CO2 estimation, contrail avoidance) are a **specialised, partly served** market. The 2027 review and any non-CO2 multiplier proposal are the next demand catalysts.

## 5. Rail and land multimodal: TSIs, Capacity Regulation, Greening Freight (W&D, Combined Transport), CountEmissionsEU, TEN-T/Rail Baltica, Single Digital Booking & Ticketing

### Takeaway

**TSIs, TEN-T, Rail Baltica**
- **[background, not re-verified]** The 2023 TSI package (Implementing Reg. (EU) 2023/1694) revised several TSIs. ### Inferences
- **Rail capacity**: buyers are ~30-40 EU IMs plus RNE/ENIM. The market is procurement-led and long-cycle, with timetable redesign (TTR) toward 2031. Software opportunities include digital capacity planning, path-request APIs, the harmonised penalty/performance system and cross-border traffic-management data exchange. Incumbents include Siemens, Hitachi, Thales/Hitachi Rail, Wabtec/Kontron, DB InfraGO tools, plus RNE's PCS/TIS **[background]**. A startup would more realistically sell to RUs and freight operators (path request automation, TAF/TAP-TSI message brokers) than to IMs directly.
- **CountEmissionsEU** is voluntary and its application is far off, so it does **not** create an immediate obligated market. It does standardise the ISO 14083 method, which **commoditises** carbon-calculation engines. Differentiation will move to primary-data capture (telematics, fuel/energy per shipment) and "verified" data reuse. Demand in 2026-2028 is driven more by large shippers' Scope 3 requests (see §6) than by the regulation.
- **W&D**: if adopted, weight allowances for zero-emission trucks and intermodal legs would add verification and enforcement needs, for example proof of an intermodal leg (which could link to eFTI data). Timing is uncertain.
- **SDBT** would create a sizeable market (distribution APIs, through-ticketing, revenue settlement between operators, platform-access compliance), but the earliest application is probably end of decade. This is a speculative market to watch, not a near-term revenue source.

## 6. Cross-cutting: ESPR/DPP, NIS2, CRA, AI Act, Data Act, Mobility Data Space, DTLF, CSRD after Omnibus and Scope 3 logistics demand

### Takeaway
Cross-cutting EU tech law hit transport in waves. **NIS2** is mostly transposed (24/27 per one tracker as of Oct 2026; 4 Member States referred to the CJEU on 8 Jul 2026), and transport (air, rail, water, road) is an Annex I "high criticality" sector. Thresholds apply to financial years from **1 Jan 2027**, and transposition is due **19 Mar 2027**. CSDDD thresholds are >5,000 employees and >€1.5bn, applying from **July 2029**. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-sustainability-omnibus-published-in-the-official-journal); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/02/eu-csddd-csrd-omnibus-published-in-official-journal-transposition-delegated-acts-and-guidelines-are-next); [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/02/omnibus-i-csrd-and-cs3d-simplification-council-of-european-union-adopts-final-text-t-simplify-sustainability-reporting-and-due-diligence-requirements/); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/eu-council-approves-omnibus-i-directive); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus)
- Revised (simplified) **ESRS** reportedly adopted 3 Jul 2026 and published 21 Sep 2026 as Delegated Reg. (EU) 2026/1563. This is a single source. — [Parallax](https://blog.thinkparallax.com/csrd-after-omnibus-where-things-stand-and-what-to-do-now)

**ESPR / Digital Product Passport**
- The Commission launched the **DPP Registry** with a testing environment; the official page is dated **11 Aug 2026**. The registry is a directory and does not store passports. — [EU Circular Economy Platform](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/node/10178)
- The **battery passport** applies from **18 Feb 2027**, the only DPP fixed in dated law. The first ESPR Working Plan (16 Apr 2025) prioritises textiles, iron and steel, aluminium, tyres, furniture and mattresses. Delegated acts for iron and steel are expected around 2026 and textiles around 2027, each followed by at least 18 months of transition. — [Bluestone PIM](https://www.bluestonepim.com/blog/digital-product-passport-timeline); [Renoon](https://www.renoon.com/blog/the-timeline-of-digital-product-passport-regulation-in-eu-espr-from-product-groups-to-rollout-schedule); [osapiens](https://osapiens.com/en/regulations/dpp-digital-product-passport)

### Inferences
- **NIS2** is the largest cross-cutting obligated population in transport: airlines, airports, ATC, IMs/RUs, shipping companies, port authorities, VTS and ITS operators. National supervision and audits ramp up in 2026-2027. Generic cyber vendors dominate, but **OT/maritime/rail-specific** offerings remain underserved: vessel OT asset inventory, port OT segmentation, rail signalling security monitoring, and supply-chain security evidence for small suppliers to NIS2 entities. The CRA (below) adds product-security demand on equipment vendors.
- **CSRD Omnibus** removes most mid-caps from mandatory reporting from FY2027, roughly an 80-90% cut on the Commission's framing. That lowers the "regulatory push" for logistics-emissions tools among SMEs. However, the remaining large shippers (and CSDDD in 2029) still request **Scope 3 Cat. 4/9 transport emissions** from carriers and forwarders. Combined with CountEmissionsEU's ISO 14083 method, this keeps demand for shipment-level emissions data, mostly as **customer-driven** (procurement/tender) demand rather than legal obligation.
- **AI Act**: the postponement gives transport AI vendors (predictive maintenance, traffic management, autonomous vessel and rail functions) a 2027-2028 runway. Compliance tooling (risk management, logging, technical documentation, conformity assessment preparation) for transport AI suppliers is a niche with a defined deadline.
- **DPP** is only indirectly a logistics market (data carriers, chain-of-custody events, reverse logistics for batteries). The battery passport (Feb 2027) is the first concrete trigger.

## 8. Ranking: obligated population, deadline imminence, software under-servedness

### Takeaway
Weighing obligated population, imminence and how underserved the software market is, the best regulation-created openings for a technical founder in late 2026 are: (1) **eFTI certified platforms and services** (9 Jul 2027; certification rules late; mostly greenfield); (2) **EUDR-to-customs/logistics data linkage** (30 Dec 2026 hard date; large population); (3) **EMSWe multi-country port-call integration** (live but unsynchronised to ~early 2027); (4) **EU Customs Reform / Data Hub data pipelines** (2028+, very large population, specs still forming); (5) **NIS2 OT security for transport operators** (large population, enforcement ramp 2026-27). ETS/FuelEU maritime, CBAM and ICS2/NCTS are large but already crowded. Rail capacity, SDBT, CountEmissionsEU and W&D are long-dated or still proposals.
