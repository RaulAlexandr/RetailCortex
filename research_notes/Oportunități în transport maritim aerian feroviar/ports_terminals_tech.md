## 1. Market size and growth of port/terminal software segments

### Takeaway
Core TOS software is a small market. Narrow "software only" estimates cluster around USD 0.4–0.6B for 2026, growing about 5–8% a year. Broader definitions that include implementation, services and automation integration claim USD 2–6B. I found no credible, methodology-transparent sizing for PCS, JIT/port-call optimization, truck appointment systems, OPS energy management or port cybersecurity as separate segments. Value for an entrant sits in adjacent, regulation- or crisis-driven niches more than in the TOS line item.

From 1 Jan 2030, FuelEU Art. 6(1) requires container and passenger ships over 5,000 GT moored more than 2h in such ports to use OPS for all electrical demand, with some exemptions until 2035 — [Zero Carbon Shipping explainer](https://zerocarbonshipping.com/news/explainer-obligations-and-opportunities-of-onshore-power)

### Inferences
- A defensible working number for licensed TOS software alone is about USD 0.4–0.6B/yr worldwide. Figures of USD 2–6B fold in services, hardware integration or automation. A founder should not size a startup on the TOS line.
- Global TOS revenue is small and concentrated (see Q2), so the money is in (a) per-terminal optimization add-ons, (b) per-transaction community or appointment fees (US drayage, EU PCS), (c) regulation-forced spend (OPS by 2029/2030, US cyber rules, EMSWe reporting) and (d) disruption-driven spend (cyber recovery, migration rescue).
- VC funding into port software is tiny relative to adjacent logistics. Portchain, Awake.AI and PortXchange have each raised roughly USD 0–12M over 6+ years (Q8). That points to slow enterprise sales cycles and small contract values, not a hot market.

### Gaps
- No credible sizing found for PCS, harbour master / port management systems, berth/yard optimization, gate OCR, truck appointment systems, equipment telematics/predictive maintenance, port digital twins, OPS energy management or port cybersecurity as discrete segments. These searches were not run because the budget ran out. Drewry's annual "Container Terminal Automation"/TOS reviews and Dynamar would be the right primary sources but were not accessible.
- No EU- vs US-specific split of TOS spend.

## 2. Incumbents and their products (share, pricing, implementation, switching costs)

### Takeaway
Kaleris (Navis N4) dominates container TOS and in 2026 is extending into AI optimization add-ons and adjacent intermodal software. Tideworks, CyberLogitec, TSB (CATOS), Solvo, RBS, Inform and Camco fill out the field, with share estimates coming only from unreliable aggregators. In the US, Advent eModal dominates truck appointments and community portals. Wabtec's Port Optimizer is the main port-level data platform at Los Angeles, with a Gothenburg deployment. In Europe, PCS are run by port-community operators (Portbase, DAKOSY and others). I found no reliable public pricing, implementation-time or market-share data.

### Inferences
- Kaleris is consolidating. It sells AI/optimization add-ons on top of N4 (the Adani deal, "Yard Intelligence Suite") and buys adjacent intermodal/depot software (Newport). A standalone yard or berth optimizer selling into N4 terminals now competes with the vendor's own upsell and depends on N4 integration access. That is a real platform risk.
- Fleet-wide deals like Adani's 15 terminals show that global terminal operators standardize on one TOS. Switching costs are therefore extreme: retraining, EDI/integration estates, equipment control interfaces and cutover risk (see Durban).
- **[Unverified background]** Navis was sold by Cargotec to Accel-KKR in 2021 and combined with XPS Technologies to form Kaleris (PE-owned). Tideworks is owned by Carrix/SSA Marine, so it is captive-affiliated, a known factor in US West Coast and Gulf terminals. Kalmar demerged from Cargotec and listed separately in mid-2024. Inform GmbH (Aachen) sells optimization modules (Syncrotess) that plug into third-party TOS. Camco (Belgium) specializes in gate and OCR automation. TBA (Netherlands) sells TEAMS TOS and simulation/emulation. Solvo originated in St Petersburg. CyberLogitec (OPUS Terminal) is Korean. Konecranes and Kalmar sell automation and equipment-control software tied to their hardware. All of this needs verification.
- **[Unverified background]** TOS pricing is typically perpetual license plus 18–22% annual maintenance, or SaaS per-move or per-TEU pricing for cloud mid-market products. Implementations commonly take 12–24 months for large container terminals. No 2026 source confirmed these numbers.

## 3. Biggest operational pain points (congestion, productivity, trucks, chassis, empties, labor, silos, migrations, cyber)

### Takeaway
In 2026 the acute pains are (1) record global congestion with more than 4.3M TEU waiting to berth in Sept 2026, (2) TOS cutover failures (Durban DGT, Aug–Oct 2026), (3) cyber incidents that take the TOS offline (Tanjung Pelepas Sept 2026, NC Ports Aug 2026), (4) US landside friction (appointments, chronic chassis-pool fragmentation, street dwell), and (5) labor contracts that cap automation, notably the ILA ban on fully automated equipment through Sept 2030.

### Inferences
- The TOS is a single point of failure. Two 2026 incidents (PTP via cyber, DGT via migration) stopped or crippled large terminals because the TOS was isolated or broken, and Transnet in 2021 had to fall back to manual. A credible TOS-independent "continuity layer" (yard state, vessel plan and gate transactions mirrored and usable offline) addresses a real, recurring loss.
- **[Unverified background]** Nagoya (NUTS terminal system) was hit by LockBit ransomware on 4 July 2023, halting container handling for about 2–3 days. DP World Australia's Nov 2023 incident halted 4 terminals for about 3 days, with about 30,000 containers stranded. The ILA struck 1–3 Oct 2024 across East/Gulf Coast ports. The ILWU contract runs to mid-2028. The US Coast Guard's final rule on cybersecurity in the Marine Transportation System was published Jan 2025 (effective mid-2025, with phased requirements). US policy since 2024 has targeted Chinese-made STS cranes (ZPMC) and their connected components, including Section 301 actions. Verify all of this.
- US East/Gulf: because the ILA contract bans full automation until 2030, labor-replacing software (gate OCR replacing clerks, autonomous equipment) is commercially risky there. Decision-support, safety and visibility tools that make the existing workforce more productive are more sellable.
- Chassis fragmentation is a data and coordination problem (who has which chassis where, street dwell, pool interoperability), not a hardware one. It is US-specific because in the US chassis are supplied by lessors/IEPs rather than carriers or truckers.

### Gaps
- No quantitative 2026 data on US truck turn times, empty-container repositioning costs, yard density or berth productivity (e.g., World Bank/S&P CPPI 2025/2026 rankings were not searched).
- No data gathered on European dockworker union stances on automation (e.g., Rotterdam, Antwerp, Hamburg strikes 2024–2026).
- Details of the ILA automation clause (definition of "semi-automated", notification/consultation requirements, the Virginia/NIT carve-outs) not verified from primary text.

## 4. What went wrong with port digitalization projects (TradeLens, PCS adoption, TOS go-live disasters)

### Takeaway
Failures come from governance and cutover risk more than from technology. TradeLens died because rival carriers would not feed data to a Maersk-controlled, profit-seeking platform. TOS go-lives fail at data migration and third-party integration, and the Aug 2026 Durban N4 cutover cut weekly throughput by 26% and pushed waits to 8–12 days. PCS adoption stalls where neutral governance is missing.

### Cited Findings
**TradeLens (IBM–Maersk), shutdown announced 30 Nov 2022 (pre-2024)**
- Official reason: the platform was technically viable, but "full global industry collaboration" was not achieved and commercial goals were missed — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2022/11/30/ibm-maersk-pull-plug-blockchain-based-tradelens-shipping-platform/); [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/Maersk-IBM-shut-down-TradeLens/637580/); [Seatrade Maritime](https://www.seatrade-maritime.com/maritime-technology/maersk-and-ibm-pull-the-plug-on-tradelens)
- Competitors were reluctant to join a platform run by a rival. Maersk led most operational aspects, and its commercial (profit) focus deepened distrust. Analysts' lesson is that independent, neutral governance is a prerequisite — [PierNext (Port of Barcelona)](https://piernext.portdebarcelona.cat/en/technology/the-closure-of-tradelens/); [Wisechain Consult](https://wisechainconsult.substack.com/p/8-why-did-tradelens-failed-and-and); [ABI Research](https://www.abiresearch.com/market-research/insight/7781608-ibm-and-maersks-tradelens-platform-discont); [NTU thesis](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/retrieve/6aa9dc48-e2a7-44ae-9a79-c796c676e6d7/ntu-113-2.pdf)
- Post-mortem material: [HBS/HBR case "Why did TradeLens fail?"](https://store.hbr.org/product/blockchain-in-container-shipping-why-did-tradelens-fail/CBS089); [FreightWaves Q&A with CargoX COO](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=481644); [The Loadstar on who will digitalize shipping next](https://theloadstar.com/remember-when-maersk-terminated-tradelens-who-will-now-take-over-digitalising-the-shipping-industry/)

**PCS adoption**
- IPCSA: smaller ports often join forces to set up a PCS or connect to a larger port's existing PCS. This is evidence that standalone PCS economics do not work at small scale — [IPCSA](https://sitemaps.ipcsa.international/pcs/pcs-general/)

### Inferences
- Pattern: a big-bang TOS cutover, during a concession or ownership change, with third-party integrations and an equipment shortfall, leads to compounding failure. Durban shows that recovery takes 6+ months and becomes a political issue.
- There is an underserved market for vendor-neutral TOS migration assurance: data-migration validation, replaying historical EDI/moves against the new TOS configuration in simulation, cutover rehearsal and go-live KPI monitoring. Integrators and TOS vendors have a conflict of interest in signalling "not ready".
- **[Unverified background]** Felixstowe (UK, Hutchison Ports) had severe disruption after its June 2020 Navis N4 go-live. TradeLens had about 150+ participants and signed MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd and ONE, but never reached profitability and went offline in early 2023. DCSA standards (eBL, track & trace, JIT port call API) became the de facto "neutral" path afterwards. Verify.
- Lesson for a startup: do not build a data-sharing network that requires competitors to trust a single commercial owner. Either be clearly neutral (port authority / association-backed, like PortXchange's Rotterdam origin) or deliver value single-player first, with network effects added later.

### Gaps

## 5. EU vs US port tech differences (landlord fragmentation, no national PCS, PCS maturity)

### Takeaway
The EU has mature, often port-authority-anchored PCS in the big northern hubs (Portbase, DAKOSY, the Antwerp ecosystem), plus regulatory harmonization (EMSWe, EU Ports Strategy 2026). Smaller EU ports lag and are steered toward joining larger PCS. The US has no national PCS. Data integration is stitched together from a private appointment/portal network (Advent eModal), port-level platforms (Port Optimizer at LA) and a voluntary federal data-sharing program (DOT FLOW). Software gaps cluster around multi-terminal landside coordination, chassis and empties.

### Cited Findings
- EU: Rotterdam (Portbase + Secure Chain), Hamburg (DAKOSY), plus Certified Pick up, S)ONE and Ci5 platforms in NL/BE — [Nexportal](https://nexportal.nl/topics/port-community-systems); [IPCSA](https://sitemaps.ipcsa.international/pcs/pcs-general/)

### Inferences
- US gap 1, cross-terminal landside orchestration: in a landlord port with 5–13 independent terminals, each with its own TOS, appointment rules and free-time policy, a drayage dispatcher has to work across all of them. eModal aggregates appointments, but optimization (which terminal, which slot, where to return the empty and pick up a chassis, dual transactions) is still largely manual for draymen. This is a natural data/ML product: an optimization layer for drayage on top of eModal, terminal sites and Port Optimizer data.
- US gap 2, chassis and empty-return intelligence: predicting where empties will be accepted and where chassis will be available, using fragmented pool and terminal data.
- US gap 3, port-authority-level visibility in operating ports (Savannah, Charleston, Virginia, Houston, NC) and in mid-size Gulf/South Atlantic ports that lack a Port Optimizer equivalent. **[Unverified background]** Many Southeast/Gulf ports (Georgia Ports Authority, SC Ports, Port of Virginia, Port Houston, Alabama State Port Authority, NC Ports) are operating ports that run their own terminals and TOS. That means a single buyer controls the whole port, which shortens sales cycles compared with West Coast landlord ports.
- EU gap: compliance-driven integration (EMSWe single-window reporting, OPS mandates, emissions reporting) for ports below the Rotterdam/Antwerp/Hamburg tier, and cross-border PCS interoperability, which shippers explicitly request.
- **[Unverified background]** EU EMSWe Regulation (EU) 2019/1239 required harmonized national single-window reporting interfaces from 15 Aug 2025. Several Member States were late. PCS operators feared disintermediation or saw it as an opportunity. Verify.

## 7. Emerging tech (AI berth/yard planning, computer vision, remote cranes, autonomy, emissions at berth, drones)

### Takeaway
AI optimization is now productized by the incumbent: Kaleris sells N4 optimization and a Yard Intelligence Suite with claimed +20% RTG and +14% truck productivity at Adani. ## 9. Where a small team can attack vs. what is locked up

### Takeaway
Locked up: core container TOS (Kaleris and peers, extreme switching costs, fleet standardization), big-hub EU PCS (port-community-owned), US appointment networks (eModal), and equipment-control/automation software (tied to Kalmar, Konecranes and ZPMC hardware). Attackable white spaces, ranked by evidence strength: (1) TOS migration assurance and simulation, (2) TOS-independent cyber/continuity layer, (3) US cross-terminal drayage, chassis and empty-return optimization, (4) EU OPS/shore-power scheduling, grid allocation, billing and compliance before the 2029/2030 deadlines, (5) probabilistic berth-availability / JIT ETA forecasting tied to emerging standards, (6) cloud TOS-lite for bulk/RoRo/multipurpose terminals at small ports.

### Inferences
White-space assessment for a data/ML/software founder (reasoned from the findings above, not sourced claims):

1. **TOS migration assurance / "terminal flight simulator" (high pain, few direct competitors).** Replay 6–12 months of historical moves, EDI (BAPLIE, COPRAR, CODECO) and gate transactions against a new TOS configuration in a discrete-event digital twin. Validate migrated data (yard inventory, holds, reefer status). Run cutover rehearsals and live go-live KPI dashboards. Buyers are terminal operators (ICTSI, DP World, APMT, Hutchison, TIL), PE owners and port authorities that grant concessions. **[Unverified background]** TBA (Konecranes) sells TOS emulation/simulation, so check overlap. Risk: needs deep TOS data-model knowledge per vendor (N4, Tideworks, CATOS).
2. **TOS-independent continuity and cyber-resilience layer.** A read-only, immutable, offline-capable mirror of yard state, vessel stowage and gate queue, plus a "manual mode" app for the gate and yard. This lets a terminal keep working, or restart faster, when the TOS is isolated. Natural pairing with US Coast Guard cyber-rule compliance for US terminals (**[Unverified background]** rule finalized Jan 2025) and with EU NIS2 (ports are "essential entities", **[Unverified background]**). Buyers are mid-size ports with small IT teams (NC Ports-type).
3. **US drayage cross-terminal optimization.** Appointment selection, dual-transaction pairing, empty-return location prediction, chassis-availability prediction and demurrage/per-diem risk scoring, sold to drayage fleets, 3PLs and BCOs (concentrated buyers, faster sales). It sits on top of eModal, terminal websites and Port Optimizer data. Risk: data access and terms-of-service scraping issues. eModal may move up-stack.
4. **EU OPS/shore-power operations software.** Berth-level OPS scheduling under grid-capacity constraints, dynamic pricing and billing (unregulated by AFIR/FuelEU), metering, and FuelEU compliance evidence for ships. Buyers are TEN-T port authorities and OPS concessionaires, with a hard 2029/2030 deadline and EU co-funding. Risk: electrical OEMs (ABB, Siemens, Schneider, **[Unverified background]**) bundle software.
5. **Probabilistic berth-availability and ETA forecasting for JIT/virtual arrival**, especially for bulk/tanker terminals (about 9% of operating time at anchor). Sell to charterers and carriers (fuel/emissions savings, EU ETS and FuelEU costs) as much as to ports. Competition: PortXchange, Portchain, Awake.AI (small), plus big data providers (**[Unverified background]** Kpler/MarineTraffic, Spire, Windward). Risk: the incentive problem (charter-party terms) limits uptake. BIMCO clause work may unlock it.
6. **Cloud TOS-lite for bulk, breakbulk, RoRo and multipurpose terminals at small/mid ports** (Adriatic, Baltic, Mediterranean, US Gulf). Container giants do not prioritize this segment, and EU SME-port funding may help. Risk: low budgets, heavy customization, long sales cycles.

Locked up or unattractive for a small team:
- Core container TOS for large terminals (Kaleris/Tideworks/CyberLogitec/TSB; fleet-wide standardization; 12–24 month implementations).
- Big-hub EU PCS (Portbase, DAKOSY, Antwerp ecosystem). These are community-owned, network-effect platforms.
- US truck appointment networks (eModal incumbency across terminals).
- Crane and automation equipment-control software (bound to OEM hardware and safety certification). Also autonomy on the US East/Gulf coast until 2030 (ILA contract).
- Industry-wide "neutral data platforms" in the TradeLens mould, unless they have authority or association backing.
