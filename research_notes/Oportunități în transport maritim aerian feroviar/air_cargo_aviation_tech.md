## 1. Air cargo market context 2025–2026 (volumes, yields, e-commerce, de minimis, EU €150 exemption, capacity, lanes, perishables)

### Takeaway
Air cargo in 2026 is growing at a mid-single-digit rate (+4.4% CTK YoY in Aug 2026) and capacity is flat, so load factors are rising. Two shocks have reshaped the market. The end of US de minimis in 2025 hit China–US e-commerce air freight hard, and the 2026 Iran war closed Gulf airspace and hubs, pushed jet fuel to near-record prices and drove fuel and war-risk surcharges. The EU replaced its €150 duty exemption with a temporary €3 flat duty from 1 July 2026. Both regimes now push the customs-data burden down to item level.

### Cited Findings
**Demand, capacity, load factors (IATA, 2026)**
- Aug 2026: global demand (CTK) +4.4% YoY; international operations +5.3% — [IATA press release, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/09-29-air-cargo-demand-grows-august/); [ATC Network summary](https://www.atc-network.com/atc-news/iata/iata-air-cargo-demand-grows-4-4-air-passenger-demand-slips-0-8-in-august); [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/airports-networks/iata-global-air-cargo-traffic-44-august)
- Aug 2026 capacity (ACTK) −0.1% YoY; cargo load factor 46.0% (+2.0 pp YoY) — [IATA Aug 2026 release](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/09-29-air-cargo-demand-grows-august/); [Cargo Solutions Network](https://cargosolutionsnetwork.com/insights/iata-air-cargo-demand-up-4-4-in-august-2026)
- Aug 2026 by carrier region: North American +6.6% (strongest), Asia-Pacific +4.3%, European +4.1%, African +3.0% — [IATA Aug 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/09-29-air-cargo-demand-grows-august/); [ATC Network](https://www.atc-network.com/atc-news/iata/iata-air-cargo-demand-grows-4-4-air-passenger-demand-slips-0-8-in-august)

Aviation Week attributes the Middle East declines to the Iran war — [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/airports-networks/iata-global-air-cargo-traffic-44-august); [Cargo Solutions Network](https://cargosolutionsnetwork.com/insights/iata-air-cargo-demand-up-4-4-in-august-2026)
- Yields: one secondary report says yields rose month-on-month in Aug 2026, the first such rise since April 2026. IATA's chief economist said strong demand and higher load factors helped airlines "recoup some of the exceptionally high fuel costs" — [Cargo Solutions Network](https://cargosolutionsnetwork.com/insights/iata-air-cargo-demand-up-4-4-in-august-2026); [IATA Aug 2026](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/09-29-air-cargo-demand-grows-august/)
- Full detailed tables are in IATA's Air Cargo Market Analysis Aug 2026 PDF (could not be fetched) — [IATA repository](https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/publications/economic-reports/air-cargo-market-analysis-august-2026/)

**2026 Iran war shock (capacity, fuel, rates)**
- Gulf airspace and airport closures removed capacity. Qatar Airways Cargo, described as the largest non-express cargo airline, suspended operations while Qatari airspace was closed — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/air-cargo-shippers-scramble-to-mitigate-iran-war-impacts)
- Freighters rerouted around Iran and Iraq add 2–4 flight hours, and the extra fuel load forces payload reductions to stay under MTOW — [Zencargo](https://www.zencargo.com/resources/middle-east-conflict-ama/)
- Jet fuel: IATA data cited by Flexport put it at $195/bbl in the week ending 27 Mar 2026. A WorldACD-based report puts it at +94% over pre-war levels — [Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/blog/middle-east-escalation-disrupts-global-ocean-and-air-freight-networks/); [WWD/Sourcing Journal](https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/logistics/air-cargo-freight-rates-iran-war-middle-east-united-airlines-cuts-capacity-fuel-costs-oil-prices-fedex-hugo-boss-1238864891/)
- Air freight rates rose by up to 70% on some routes, per Reuters. Xeneta attributed much of the increase to lost capacity at Middle East transshipment hubs rather than to fuel alone — [Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/air-freight-rates-soar-middle-040444434.html); [WWD](https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/logistics/air-cargo-freight-rates-iran-war-middle-east-united-airlines-cuts-capacity-fuel-costs-oil-prices-fedex-hugo-boss-1238864891/)
- Carriers added fuel and war-risk surcharges, e.g. United Cargo's "Market Disruption Fee" and an Air Canada Cargo carrier surcharge. Cathay Cargo reviews its surcharges every two weeks — [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/air-cargo-carriers-add-fuel-surcharges-driven-by-iran-war/818762/)
- Rate volatility in normal times is also high: Freightos says a single lane's rates can swing up to 80% within a week (vendor claim) — [Freightos](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/air-cargo-digital-booking-ai-multimodal/)

**End of US de minimis (2025) and e-commerce flows**
- Before 2 May 2025, e-commerce was about half of all China–US air cargo shipments. Low-value e-commerce shipments from China to the US fell 43% between April and end-May 2025 (Reuters, via SupplyChainBrain) — [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/42094-end-to-de-minimis-slows-flow-of-air-cargo-from-china-to-us)
- Reported drop sizes conflict because they measure different things. One trade outlet cites a ~60% fall in transpacific air cargo volume (tariffs plus de minimis), and another cites a ~30% cut in China→US capacity within weeks — [SmartKargo blog](https://www.smartkargo.com/blog/how-the-end-of-de-minimis-is-reshaping-cross-border-air-cargo-for-airlines); [SCW Mag](https://scw-mag.com/news/de-minimis-end-to-trigger-air-cargo-turbulence/); [Lars Winkelbauer](https://www.larswinkelbauer.com/writing/end-of-de-minimis/) (exact attribution of each figure among these three was not verified)
- Temu daily active users reportedly fell 52% and Shein's 25% between March and May 2025. Both expanded US warehousing to import in bulk, and tariffs on some of their goods were cut from a 145% peak to as low as 30% — same source set as above ([SmartKargo](https://www.smartkargo.com/blog/how-the-end-of-de-minimis-is-reshaping-cross-border-air-cargo-for-airlines); [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/42094-end-to-de-minimis-slows-flow-of-air-cargo-from-china-to-us))
- Baseline: the US processed more than 1.36 billion de minimis shipments (≤$800) in the fiscal year before the change. A figure of about 600k Shein+Temu packages per day, attributed to a House committee, appears only on a low-reliability blog — [Red Stag Fulfillment](https://redstagfulfillment.com/how-many-packages-are-sent-under-de-minimis/)
- Aug 2026's +13.2% YoY on Asia–North America (above) is measured against a de-minimis-depressed Aug 2025 base — [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/airports-networks/iata-global-air-cargo-traffic-44-august)

**EU end of €150 customs duty exemption (2026)**
- The EU removed the €150 duty exemption on 1 July 2026 and replaced it with a temporary €3 flat customs duty, expected to run until 1 July 2028, when the EU Customs Data Hub and standard tariff treatment are due to take over. Several sources cite Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382 as the legal basis — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2025/11/eu-end-150-customs-duty-exemption-2026.html); [Zonos](https://zonos.com/blog/eu-customs-reform-2026); [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/eu-parcel-duty-150-exemption/); [DHL](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-vn/news-and-insights/eu-customs-changes-2026)
- **Conflict on scope:** one source says the €3 applies only to IOSS-registered and postal consignments, with non-IOSS commercial shipments under €150 paying the standard tariff. Others say it applies to all B2C parcels from non-EU sellers, including those under IOSS. Sources also disagree on whether it is charged per item, per tariff line or per HS-code line — [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/eu-parcel-duty-150-exemption/); [Zonos](https://zonos.com/blog/eu-customs-reform-2026); [UK business.gov.uk](https://www.business.gov.uk/campaign/europe/european-union-eu-regulations/eu-ucc-reforms/)
- One source reports a further €2-per-item handling fee from November 2026 (not corroborated) — [search summary of EU customs blogs; e.g. nShift](https://nshift.com/blog/eu-customs-changes-ecommerce-2026); [AVASK](https://avask.com/blog/eu-customs-duty-2026-new-rules/)
- Temu's EU order growth was reportedly above 60% in May 2026, and one analyst argues the fee will not slow Temu or Shein in 2026 (opinion, not measured data) — [ShopAppy](https://shopappy.com/trade/cross-border-commerce/eu-de-minimis-wont-slow-temu-shein); [SupplyChainBrain](https://www.supplychainbrain.com/articles/43004-eu-to-end-de-minimis-with-new-customs-fee-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Mid-single-digit demand growth with flat capacity points to a tight market in late 2026. Yield and surcharge management, and fast re-pricing, are worth more than in 2023–24.
- E-commerce volume has not gone away. It has shifted to bulk imports into US warehouses and toward Europe, Latin America and other destinations. Air cargo demand now responds to policy changes in weeks, which favours tools that model policy scenarios and do lane-level demand sensing.
- The US and EU regimes now both need item-level data (HS code, value, parties) on low-value parcels. That is a large classification, validation and data-quality problem, suited to an ML-capable team.
- Middle East capacity loss and rerouting showed how fragile network planning is. Payload and route optimisation, plus surcharge pass-through, became urgent in 2026.

- Current status of the Iran war and Gulf hub reopening (Qatar, Dubai) as of Oct 2026: the sources span months, so whether capacity has recovered is unknown.
- Primary text of Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382 not checked, so the €3 scope remains disputed.
- **[BACKGROUND – UNVERIFIED]** US de minimis was suspended for China/HK on 2 May 2025 and for all countries on 29 Aug 2025 (Executive Order of 30 Jul 2025). Postal operators in many countries temporarily stopped US-bound parcel services in Aug–Sep 2025.

## 2. Air cargo digital sales, booking, pricing and revenue management

### Takeaway
Digital spot booking via marketplaces (WebCargo by Freightos, cargo.one) has reached most airline capacity by coverage, around 80% per WebCargo. Real digital penetration of transactions is far lower and not independently measured. In 2026 the leaders are moving from booking toward "AI operating systems" covering both air and ocean. Airline-side revenue management and cargo management systems remain an incumbent oligopoly.

### Cited Findings
- WebCargo by Freightos (2026 webinar): digital booking covers 80 airlines and about 80% of global capacity. An earlier Freightos article claims over 1 million bookings a year and 70+ airlines and GSAs live, more than 70% of capacity (vendor claims) — [WebCargo webinar](https://www.webcargo.co/resources/air-cargo-digital-transformation-webinar/); [Freightos](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/air-cargo-digital-booking-ai-multimodal/)
- Wikipedia notes that by 2024 the carriers on Freightos/WebCargo represented about 70% of global cargo capacity, but not all of that capacity is digitally bookable — [Wikipedia: Freightos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freightos)
- WebCargo is still adding carriers: Air Europa (Europe–LatAm network) and Euro Cargo Aviation (dynamic, contract and spot rates with eBooking) — [Freightos PR, Air Europa](https://www.freightos.com/press-release/air-europa-joins-webcargo-by-freightos-platform-expanding-digital-air-cargo-access-in-european-and-latin-american-markets/); [Freightos PR, Euro Cargo Aviation](https://www.freightos.com/press-release/euro-cargo-aviation-partners-with-webcargo-to-deliver-real-time-air-freight-rates-and-instant-ebooking/)

### Inferences
- Coverage is close to saturated. The spot-booking marketplace layer is effectively a duopoly (WebCargo, cargo.one) plus CargoAi, with airline-owned portals alongside. A new marketplace entrant faces a closed network-effect market.
- The pivot to "AI OS / multimodal" by both leaders suggests booking fees alone do not justify the valuations. Value is moving to workflow (quote-to-book-to-track-to-invoice) and to rate data.
- Air is still well behind ocean on contract and allotment digitisation, capacity forecasting for GSAs and smaller carriers, and charter brokering. Rates swing up to 80% a week and surcharges are re-set every two weeks, which makes pricing intelligence valuable. Incumbent RM vendors mainly serve large carriers.

## 3. IATA ONE Record, e-AWB, e-freight, Cargo iQ, CO2 calculation

### Takeaway
ONE Record became IATA's preferred, but voluntary, air cargo data standard on 1 January 2026. It is not a mandatory cut-over. Airlines carrying about 70% of AWB volume were reportedly "on track", but legacy Cargo-IMP and Cargo-XML still run alongside, and regulators do not yet broadly accept ONE Record data. The industry will be running mixed legacy and ONE Record systems for years, so translation layers between them will be needed.

### Cited Findings
- ONE Record became the preferred standard from 1 Jan 2026, but stays voluntary. During the transition some partners use ONE Record and others legacy systems; Cargo-IMP and Cargo-XML remain in use — [GoFreight](https://gofreight.com/blog/air-cargo-digitalization); [Nexportal](https://nexportal.nl/news/iata-one-record-standard-2026); [Transinfo](https://www.transinfo.ge/en/news/iata-one-record-air-cargo-2026)
- IATA reportedly said airlines carrying more than 70% of global AWB volume were on track. CargoForwarder gives 72% — [CargoForwarder Global, Sep 2025](https://cargoforwarder.eu/2025/09/21/one-record-building-momentum-for-2026/)
- IATA's December 2025 survey: over 70% of respondents aware of ONE Record, and nearly half reported being prepared — [Stattimes](https://www.stattimes.com/air-cargo/more-than-70-of-air-cargo-industry-are-aware-of-one-record-iata-1357429); [Payload Asia](https://payloadasia.com/2025/12/iata-survey-shows-growing-awareness-one-record-areas-further-support/)
- Cathay Cargo was the first carrier to adopt ONE Record, in December 2024 — [Air Cargo Week](https://aircargoweek.com/cathay-cargo-becomes-first-carrier-to-adopt-iatas-one-record-data-protocol/)
- Governments do not yet broadly accept ONE Record data directly in regulatory filings, and IATA lists this as a next step — [GoFreight](https://gofreight.com/blog/air-cargo-digitalization)
- Vendor and consultancy positioning around a "2026 pivot year" (e.g., IOG) — [Impactograph](https://impactograph.com/iata-one-record-as-the-air-cargo-data-backbone-why-2026-is-the-pivot-year/)

### Inferences
- "Awareness 70%, prepared ~50%" points to a long tail of small forwarders, GSAs and ground handlers who will need cheap ONE Record adapters (API servers, mapping from Cargo-IMP/XML, event-quality monitoring). Large carriers will buy from incumbent CMS vendors, but the long tail is underserved.
- Until customs authorities accept ONE Record (ICS2, ACAS), every party has to keep dual pipelines. Bridging middleware that turns ONE Record data into regulatory filings is a real product gap.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: current e-AWB penetration rate, e-freight status, Cargo iQ membership and milestones, and the IATA CO2 calculation methodology for cargo (CO2 Connect) or EU CountEmissionsEU / ISO 14083 adoption.
- No list of airlines or forwarders actually live in production on ONE Record in 2026. The "100+ stakeholders in production" claim seen in one blog could not be traced to IATA.
- **[BACKGROUND – UNVERIFIED]** IATA made e-AWB the default contract of carriage on enabled lanes from 2019. The EU CountEmissionsEU proposal (2023) would standardise transport emissions calculation on ISO 14083. Status in 2026 not checked.

## 4. Air cargo security and customs regulation (EU ICS2, US ACAS, TSA) and the software demand it creates

### Takeaway
Both the EU and US have significantly raised advance-data requirements. In the EU, ICS2 has required house-level ENS filing by forwarders for air cargo since March 2023, Release 3 has extended it to all modes, and multiple filing across modes is planned for H2 2026. In the US, CBP's Enhanced ACAS interim final rule (effective 21 Nov 2025) adds mandatory and conditional data elements aimed at e-commerce. Phased enforcement now reportedly runs to 1 May 2027. Compliance data quality, verified-consignor programmes and filing middleware are concrete, deadline-driven demand.

### Cited Findings
**EU ICS2**
- ICS2 Release 2 has covered air cargo since March 2023. The air carrier files the master ENS. Forwarders and consolidators that issue house air waybills must file house-level ENS data. PLACI (Pre-Loading Advance Cargo Information), a minimum dataset, must be filed before loading on an EU-bound aircraft — [Rhenus](https://www.rhenus.group/news-media/en/what-freight-forwarders-need-to-know-about-ics2/); [Customs Support Group](https://www.customssupport.com/eu-customs-pre-arrival-security-and-safety-program-ics2/); [UPS](https://www.ups.com/us/en/supplychain/resources/news-and-market-updates/import-control-system-2)
- **Conflicting Release 3 dates:** one source says ICS2 became the sole system for all modes on 1 Sep 2025, another says road and rail started on 1 Apr 2025, and a third says ICS2 v3 became mandatory for all modes on 3 Feb 2026 with v2 decommissioned. Road and rail derogations for Poland, Romania and Slovakia reportedly ran to 1 June 2026 — [iCustoms](https://www.icustoms.ai/blogs/ics2-release-3-guide-for-freight-forwarders/); [Zollagentur](https://zollagentur.gmbh/en/compliance/ics2-phase-3/); [TradeTech](https://site.tradetech.net/china-to-europe-ics2-ens-rail-filings-guide-for-forwarders-and-nvoccs/)
- Multiple filing is expected to extend to all transport modes in H2 2026, subject to European Commission confirmation — [GoFreight compliance calendar](https://gofreight.com/blog/freight-forwarder-compliance-calendar)

**US Enhanced ACAS**
- The CBP Enhanced ACAS interim final rule took effect 21 Nov 2025 and amends 19 CFR Parts 103 and 122 — [Federal Register / GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-21/html/2025-20606.htm); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/21/2025-20606/enhanced-air-cargo-advance-screening-acas)
- It adds 5 new mandatory data elements at the lowest AWB level, 12 conditional elements when the shipper is not a verified known consignor, and 4 more elements required under certain conditions. Examples of new mandatory fields include consignee email and phone. The aim is a fuller picture of parties, financial data and relationships, especially for e-commerce — [Expeditors](https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/cbp-announces-updates-to-air-cargo-advance-screening-requirements); [Eckert Seamans](https://www.eckertseamans.com/?p=25983); [STR Trade Report, Nov](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/november/more-data-required-for-air-cargo-shipments)
- FreightWaves headline: "Air cargo faces $1Bn overhaul to comply with new CBP rule" (cost estimate, details not retrieved) — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=567975)
- Enforcement: the original plan was 12 months of enforcement discretion with full enforcement on 21 Nov 2026. A later update says CBP extended phased enforcement to 1 May 2027, subject to conditions — [Eckert Seamans](https://www.eckertseamans.com/?p=25983); [STR Trade Report, Aug](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/august/cbp-delays-full-enforcement-of-expanded-data-requirements-for-air-cargo-shipments); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/enhanced-air-cargo-acas-cbp/)
- The comment period closed 20 Jan 2026. A tracker lists a "final rule" on 22 Jan 2026, which is suspicious given the comment timing and needs checking — [FRTracker](https://frtracker.app/fr/2025-20606)
- Carriers are issuing customer FAQs (e.g., FedEx ACAS FAQ, Oct 2026), and an OMB information-collection request (Feb 2026) exists — [FedEx ACAS FAQ](https://www.fedex.com/content/dam/fedex/meisa-middle-east/downloads/fedex-acas-faqs-oct-26-en-meisa.pdf); [OMB ICR](https://omb.report/icr/202602-1651-001/doc/173039200.pdf); [CBP Enhanced ACAS FAQ, Aug 2024](https://cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-08/enhanced_acas_faq_cbp_08-2024_-_508_complete.pdf)

### Inferences
- Demand comes from many filers: every house-AWB issuer (forwarders, e-commerce consolidators, postal operators) on EU and US inbound flows must produce accurate, item-level party and goods data before loading. The "verified known consignor" path in Enhanced ACAS creates a market for KYC and verification of shippers.
- Hard ML problems with a compliance buyer include: plain-language goods description checks (ICS2 and ACAS reject vague descriptions), HS classification, consignee and party entity resolution and validation, and risk pre-screening against "do not load" referrals.
- The hard deadlines (ACAS phased enforcement to May 2027, ICS2 multiple filing in H2 2026, EU €3 duty from July 2026) give a small team a sales clock.
- Incumbent filers include CargoWise, Descartes, Champ, carrier portals and customs brokers, but mid-size e-commerce consolidators and smaller forwarders on the China→EU and China→US lanes have to fix data quality upstream of filing.

### Gaps

## 11. White spaces a small technical team could attack vs. areas locked up by incumbents

### Takeaway
The best wedges for a data/ML founder are deadline-driven data-quality and compliance problems created by 2025–2027 rule changes, and document AI for airworthiness records and parts authenticity after AOG Technics. The rule changes are US Enhanced ACAS (phased enforcement to May 2027), EU ICS2 multiple filing (H2 2026), the EU €3 per-item duty (from July 2026) and ONE Record dual-running. These problems are new, hard (messy unstructured data) and have a compliance budget behind them. Core systems (cargo management, airline RM, AODB/A-CDM, M&E ERP, crew/ops) and the spot-booking marketplace layer are locked up.

### Cited Findings
- Enhanced ACAS adds 5 mandatory, 12 conditional and 4 situational data elements at the lowest AWB level, aimed at e-commerce. Full or phased enforcement is reportedly pushed to 1 May 2027 — [Expeditors](https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/cbp-announces-updates-to-air-cargo-advance-screening-requirements); [STR, Aug](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/august/cbp-delays-full-enforcement-of-expanded-data-requirements-for-air-cargo-shipments)
- FreightWaves frames the compliance cost as a "$1Bn overhaul" — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=567975)
- ICS2 requires house-level ENS from HAWB issuers and PLACI before loading. Multiple filing is expected to extend in H2 2026 — [Rhenus](https://www.rhenus.group/news-media/en/what-freight-forwarders-need-to-know-about-ics2/); [GoFreight](https://gofreight.com/blog/freight-forwarder-compliance-calendar)
- The EU €3 flat duty from 1 Jul 2026 may be charged per item or per tariff line (disputed), which requires item-level classification — [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/eu-parcel-duty-150-exemption/); [Zonos](https://zonos.com/blog/eu-customs-reform-2026)
- ONE Record is voluntary and about half of respondents are prepared. Legacy formats persist, and regulators do not accept ONE Record directly — [Payload Asia](https://payloadasia.com/2025/12/iata-survey-shows-growing-awareness-one-record-areas-further-support/); [GoFreight](https://gofreight.com/blog/air-cargo-digitalization)
- Digital booking coverage is about 80% of capacity at WebCargo, and cargo.one is consolidating multimodal — [WebCargo](https://www.webcargo.co/resources/air-cargo-digital-transformation-webinar/); [Stattimes](https://www.stattimes.com/air-cargo/cargoone-acquires-cargofive-launches-ai-freight-os-1358241)
- AOG Technics: 60,000+ parts with forged ARCs sold over 4+ years — [UK SFO](https://www.gov.uk/sfo-cases/aog-technics-ltd)
- Core M&E is concentrated in AMOS, TRAX, Ramco and IFS, and the market is $7.5–9.5B — [Guideflow](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/aviation-mro-software); [Straits Research](https://straitsresearch.com/report/aviation-mro-software-market)
- Rate volatility of up to 80% a week and surcharges reviewed every two weeks — [Freightos](https://www.freightos.com/freight-resources/air-cargo-digital-booking-ai-multimodal/); [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/air-cargo-carriers-add-fuel-surcharges-driven-by-iran-war/818762/)

### Inferences
**Attackable (ranked by fit for a data/ML/software founder):**
1. **Pre-filing compliance data-quality engine for e-commerce air shipments (EU and US).** This covers goods-description normalisation, HS classification, consignee and party validation (email, phone, address), known-consignor evidence, and risk pre-scoring before ICS2 ENS, ACAS and EU €3 duty calculation. Buyers are e-commerce consolidators, mid-size forwarders and postal/express hubs (Liège, Leipzig, LAX/ONT/JFK/MIA gateways). It sits upstream of existing filing tools and integrates with them rather than replacing them. The rule changes create the urgency, and the enforcement dates (May 2027 ACAS, H2 2026 ICS2 multiple filing) set the sales timeline.
2. **ONE Record bridge for the long tail.** A hosted ONE Record server plus Cargo-IMP/XML mapping plus data-quality monitoring for SME forwarders, GSAs and ground handlers, eventually feeding regulatory filings. The risk is that IATA or CMS incumbents bundle this for free, so it should be priced low and kept as an early wedge.
3. **Airworthiness document AI and parts provenance.** Automated verification of release certificates (Form 1 / 8130-3), consistency checks against back-to-birth records, and anomaly detection across a distributor's or MRO's receiving dock. Buyers are lessors, MROs and parts distributors. AOG Technics is the reference case for the sales pitch. Expect certification and liability barriers. Sell the product as decision support, not as the release authority.
4. **Pricing and capacity intelligence for smaller carriers, GSAs and charter brokers.** Big carriers buy PROS, Accelya or IBS, but GSAs and charter brokers price from spreadsheets. Weekly 80% swings and fortnightly surcharges create demand for forecasting and quote-optimisation tools.
5. **Disruption and rerouting decision support (payload/fuel/route, cargo re-protection)** for freighter operators, given the 2026 airspace closures and fuel at $195/bbl.

**Locked up / avoid head-on:** airline cargo management systems (IBS iCargo, Champ, Unisys, SmartKargo), airline RM (PROS, Accelya), multi-airline booking marketplaces (WebCargo, cargo.one, CargoAi), forwarder ERP (CargoWise), AODB/A-CDM/CUPPS/baggage (SITA, Amadeus, Collins), M&E ERP (AMOS, TRAX, Ramco, IFS), and crew/flight ops (Jeppesen/Thoma Bravo, Lufthansa Systems, CAE). Background knowledge on incumbents beyond the cited M&E and marketplace players is unverified. Long procurement cycles, safety certification and integration lock-in make these unattractive for a small team.

- Competitive density in compliance data-quality tooling (e.g., iCustoms, Descartes and newer AI customs startups) was not mapped, so how crowded the space is remains unknown.
- Pricing and willingness-to-pay data for any of the white spaces was not found.
