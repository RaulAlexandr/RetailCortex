## 1. San Pedro Bay (LA/LB) and Oakland: volumes 2023–2026, tariff-driven swings, terminals, automation/ILWU, digital platforms, chassis, warehousing

### Takeaway
After a tariff whiplash (a front-loaded 2025 record, a dip in early 2026 and a sharp rebound from mid-2026), San Pedro Bay hit its highest monthly volumes ever in September 2026: LA had 1,042,652 TEU and LB 997,177 TEU, together about 2.04M TEU in one month. Empties also hit a record, which points to severe imbalance. Oakland is flat to down YTD. Automation has stalled under ILWU pressure (LBCT and TraPac are still the only fully automated yards). The ports' own data platforms (POLA Port Optimizer, POLB Supply Chain Information Highway) are free and port-sponsored, so a startup should build on top of them, not against them.

### Inferences
- The volume series is extremely policy-driven: about −11% YoY in Jan, +31.7% in May, then all-time records in Jun–Sep at LB. **Forecasting import surges from tariff-calendar events** (deadlines, truce expiries, court rulings) is a concrete ML product need for BCOs, drayage carriers, chassis pools and IE warehouses. Even NRF's late-2025 forecast missed the H2 2026 surge.
- The record empties (371k TEU at POLA in Sept 2026, ~36% of throughput by computation: 371,206 / 1,042,652) and an export/import ratio of ~0.23 (125,751 / 545,696) suggest **empty-repositioning and street-turn matching** remains a large, under-served optimization problem.
- No public chassis-utilization data plus surge-driven chassis tightness (DCLI pulling back out-of-service units) points to a data gap. An independent **chassis/turn-time telemetry layer** (from ELD/GPS of drayage fleets) could be sold to BCOs, pools and ports.
- Port data platforms are free and port-run (POLB SCIH, POLA Port Optimizer). That leaves the opportunity in **workflow apps on top of them** (exception management, appointment orchestration, demurrage/detention prevention), not in competing as the "port community system".
- With IE availability near 13% and tenants holding leverage, **flexible/short-term warehousing and transload capacity marketplaces** have supply. Demand is spiky because of front-loading.
- Automation is slow on the waterside (ILWU contract until 2028, see gaps), so productivity gains must come from the landside and from data (truck flows, yard planning visibility, gate optimization) rather than terminal robotics.

## 2. Competition: share shift to East/Gulf Coast, Pacific Northwest, Canada (Prince Rupert/Vancouver) and Mexican Pacific ports

### Takeaway
West Coast share swung month to month in 2026. East and Gulf ports led in March (44.3% vs 38.3%). By September the top-5 West Coast ports had 46.7% vs 39.7% for East and Gulf, helped by Suez and Panama problems and LA/LB's surge. Mexico's Pacific ports keep growing (Manzanillo +9.9% H1 2026, ~3.16M TEU in 9 months), which strengthens a Mexico-to-US rail/truck feeder alternative and a nearshoring channel.

### Inferences
- Shippers now route-shop month to month (West vs East/Gulf vs Mexico vs Canada) based on tariff timing, canal reliability and rail rates. **Multi-gateway landed-cost and transit-time comparison** that updates with policy events is a product gap. Today this is mostly done in forwarders' spreadsheets.
- Mexico's Pacific ports are growing faster than US West Coast ports YTD (Manzanillo ~+10% vs LA ~+3%). Combined with a 72/28 road/rail split at Lázaro Cárdenas, this opens **cross-border visibility (Mexican port → rail/truck → Laredo/US)** and origin/USMCA compliance tooling.
- With East/Gulf share able to swing ~5 points in a month, LA/LB demand is volatile. Variable-capacity products (on-demand drayage, flex warehousing) suit this better than fixed contracts.

## 3. California environmental regulation driving tech demand (ACF, At-Berth, CHE, CAAP, ISR, SB 253/261)

### Takeaway
The headline zero-emission truck mandate has collapsed. CARB withdrew its EPA waiver request in January 2025, settled, and filed a repeal of the drayage and high-priority-fleet parts of Advanced Clean Fleets before a court-ordered 31 August 2026 deadline. Compliance work has **moved to a fragmented set of port-level and voluntary programs** rather than disappearing. Rules that don't need a waiver still bite: the At-Berth Regulation (RoRo and LA/LB tankers from 2025, all CA tankers from 2027) and SB 253 (Scope 1–2 first due 10 Aug 2026, Scope 3 from 2027). SB 261 remains enjoined.

### Cited Findings
**Advanced Clean Fleets (ACF) — drayage**
- CARB **withdrew its EPA waiver request in January 2025**. As a result, CARB **cannot currently enforce the high-priority-fleet and drayage provisions against private fleets**, although the rule was technically still on the books — [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/advanced-clean-fleets-regulation-requirements-and-exemptions/) (secondary/legal explainer)
- CARB agreed in a 2025 legal settlement to repeal the drayage and high-priority-fleet parts. The repeal of the private and drayage portions was filed with the Office of Administrative Law (file **2026-0729-02**) **ahead of a court-ordered 31 August 2026 deadline**. OAL review is the last step before removal — [Advanced BioFuels USA, Sept 2026](https://advancedbiofuelsusa.info/carb-files-acf-repeal-ahead-of-court-ordered-august-31-deadline); [Power Progress](https://www.powerprogress.com/news/carb-repeals-advanced-clean-fleets-rules/8057220.article); [FreightWaves: "California guts key Advanced Clean Fleet rules; what's next?"](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=566262); [FreightWaves: "California deal with 16 states would end key parts of ACF"](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=535616)
- Container News: the **"drayage zero-emission mandate collapse leaves port fleets with a new compliance puzzle"**. The workload has shifted to "a longer list of overlapping port-level and voluntary programs", and drayage operators still need to check port truck-registry requirements — [Container News](https://container-news.com/drayage-zero-emission-mandate-collapse-leaves-port-fleets-with-a-new-compliance-puzzle/)
- CARB **delayed registration requirements for two new programs** — [J.J. Keller](https://jjkellercompliancenetwork.com/news/carb-delays-registration-requirements-for-two-new-programs) (date and program names not confirmed)
- Earlier context: diesel trucks took up the EV slack to meet cargo demand in California — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=494447)
- **Conflict:** one 2026 search summary says the manufacturer-side Advanced Clean Trucks rule "remains in effect". That conflicts with the 2025 Congressional Review Act resolutions revoking California's ACT waiver (see Gaps). Treat ACT status as unresolved/litigated.

**At-Berth Regulation (ocean-going vessels)**
- **RoRo vessels and tankers at LA/LB terminals had to comply from 1 Jan 2025. Tankers at all other California terminals follow from 1 Jan 2027.** Compliance is via shore power (OPS) or a CARB-approved exhaust capture-and-control system. CARB enforces through at-berth inspections and ongoing audits of terminal reports, and issues Notices of Violation under its penalty policy — [Offshore Energy](https://offshore-energy.biz/?p=740761); [Gard](https://www.gard.no/insights/reducing-air-pollution-from-ships-in-california-ports); [DNV via Port Technology](https://www.porttechnology.org/news/dnv-expands-emissions-regulations-in-californian-ports/); [ABS](https://ww2.eagle.org/en/rules-and-resources/regulatory-updates/CARB-at-Berth.html)
- A March 2026 CARB revised terminal plan (PBF Martinez refinery terminal) confirms the 2027 tanker schedule, so terminal compliance plans are being filed now — [CARB PDF, Mar 2026](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/2026_03_CARB%20Revised%20Terminal%20Plan%20signed_PBF%20Martinez.pdf)
- EPA authorized California's At-Berth rule, so unlike the truck rules it is not exposed to waiver revocation — [Maritime Executive](https://maritime-executive.com/article/epa-clears-california-s-rules-for-ships-to-eliminate-at-berth-emissions)
- An older FreightWaves piece describes a proposal phasing tankers from 2027 (LA/LB) and 2029 (elsewhere). That proposal was **superseded** by the adopted schedule above — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/california-plans-to-expand-rules-requiring-ships-to-use-shore-power)

**SB 253 / SB 261 (climate disclosure)**
- 18 Nov 2025: the Ninth Circuit **enjoined enforcement of SB 261** (climate-risk reports) pending appeal, while **SB 253 (GHG emissions) was allowed to proceed**. Oral argument was set for 9 Jan 2026. ## 4. US federal factors: tariffs on China, US–China truce, Section 301 vessel fees, de minimis, UFLPA, CBP ACE, FMC

### Takeaway
2026 has been a legal and tariff whiplash year:
1. The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs (early 2026), which triggered refunds through a CBP "CAPE" process in ACE. Refunds are not automatic.
2. A 10% Section 122 surcharge ran from ~24 Feb to its 150-day limit on 24 July 2026. The CIT ruled it unlawful on 7 May, but a Federal Circuit stay kept it in force.
3. New Section 301 duties replaced it from 24 July.
4. The US–China truce (Kuala Lumpur/Busan, Oct 2025) was extended to 10 Jan 2027.
5. The Section 301 fees on Chinese-built/operated vessels (live 14 Oct 2025, suspended 10 Nov 2025) are suspended until 9 Nov 2026, with a USTR proposal to extend to 9 Jan 2027.

Together this has made **tariff and refund computation a top operational pain**.

**IEEPA ruling, refunds, Section 122 and replacements**

## 8. Biggest operational pain points where software/data/ML can help

### Takeaway
Ranked by late-2026 urgency (inferred from the facts above):
1. **Tariff, duty and refund computation** amid constant regime changes (IEEPA refunds via CAPE, possible Section 122 refunds, new 301 tariffs, truce and vessel-fee cliffs on 9–10 Nov 2026 and 9–10 Jan 2027).
2. **Surge-driven landside congestion**: record imports and record empties, chassis tightness with no public utilization data, appointment fragmentation.
3. **Fragmented emissions compliance**: port-level programs after the ACF collapse, At-Berth for tankers 2027, SB 253 Scope 3 in 2027.
4. **Origin and transshipment compliance** under China+1.

### Cited Findings
- Tariff refunds require active claims (CAPE in ACE), and Section 122 refunds hinge on the appeal, so importers are told to keep **entry-level records of every payment** — [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/tariffs-redux-what-importers-should-know-about-ieepa-refunds-and-section-122/); [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/ieepa-tariff-refunds-frequently-asked-questions)
- Multiple November deadlines (vessel-fee suspension expiry 9 Nov, Section 301 exclusions, truce extension paperwork) were live in late Oct 2026 — [Worldwide Logistics](https://worldwidelogisticsltd.com/2026/10/28/u-s-china-trade-important-november-deadlines-approach/); [US Tariff Rates](https://ustariffrates.com/news/section-301-double-deadline-november-9-port-fees-exclusions)
- The late-July 2026 tariff change produced an early peak and front-loading — [NRF](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/import-cargo-s-extended-peak-season-wrapping-up)
- POLA empties hit a record 371k TEU in Sept 2026 — [gCaptain/POLA](https://gcaptain.com/port-of-los-angeles-sets-all-time-cargo-record-as-imports-surge/)
- Chassis-pool utilization is not public at any gateway. DCLI added capacity by repairing out-of-service units during the surge — [Watchdesk](https://buttondown.com/watchdesk/archive/triple-14-ports-2026-09-14-7492/); [JOC](https://magazine.joc.com/august-3-2026/0575629001784915593/p39)
- After the ACF repeal, drayage compliance is spread across overlapping port-level and voluntary programs — [Container News](https://container-news.com/drayage-zero-emission-mandate-collapse-leaves-port-fleets-with-a-new-compliance-puzzle/)
- AI customs startup Amari AI says its software automates customs data entry and paperwork and **monitors rule changes, updating analysis when tariffs or classifications shift**. That validates classification and landed-cost churn as a paid pain point — [Dealroom/press, Feb 2026](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/ex-google-and-linkedin-engineers-raise-4-5m-for-ai-customs-broker-startup-amid-trump-tariffs)

### Inferences
Product ideas (founder-relevant: data/ML/software), each tied to evidence above:
1. **Duty refund and reconciliation engine**: ingest ACE reports (ES-003, entry summaries), 7501s and broker files; compute IEEPA, Section 122 and 301 exposure by entry; prepare CAPE claims and protests; track liquidation dates. Contingency-fee pricing. Window: now to roughly 2027.
2. **Policy-aware landed-cost and scenario simulator**: an HTS-level rate stack (232/301/new-301/AD-CVD/MPF/HMF), origin rules, truce/fee cliffs, plus mode/gateway alternatives (LA vs Savannah vs Manzanillo + rail). Flexport, Zonos, Avalara and Descartes compete here, so differentiate on gateway/routing and timing (when to pull forward).
3. **Import-surge forecasting** for drayage carriers, chassis pools, IE warehouses and terminals. Inputs: AIS, booking/BOL data (US import manifests are public via CBP AMS-derived datasets), the tariff calendar and NRF/Descartes signals.
4. **Empty-container / street-turn / chassis matching** built on port data (SCIH, Port Optimizer) plus fleet telematics.
5. **Drayage compliance aggregator**: one dashboard for port truck registry, CTF, CARB Clean Truck Check, incentive eligibility and ZE charging. Mandate risk is lower now, but complexity remains.
6. **Scope 3 transport emissions API** (ISO 14083) for SB 253 2027 filers with transpacific supply chains.
7. **Origin and transshipment risk scoring** (supplier graph + BOL data) for UFLPA, new 301 and Vietnam transshipment exposure.
8. **AI-hardware air logistics control tower** (Taiwan/Mexico → US data centers).

### Gaps

- Cybersecurity incidents at West Coast ports in 2025–2026 (e.g., US Coast Guard concerns about Chinese-made ZPMC cranes, NDAA crane provisions) were not retrieved.
- ZEV truck charging logistics (charger counts at/near San Pedro Bay, e.g., WattEV, Forum Mobility, TeraWatt sites) were not retrieved for 2026.

### Inferences
- The funding pattern (small AI customs seeds in 2025–2026, no big drayage rounds) suggests **investors favor compliance and document-AI with clear ROI** over asset-light marketplaces after Convoy. A technical founder can enter with a narrow agentic workflow (classification, entry prep, refund claims) and expand into landed-cost and routing.
- Gaps visible from the market:
  - (1) **Corridor-specific surge forecasting** sold to landside players (none found).
  - (2) **Chassis/empty matching** with real utilization data (none public).
