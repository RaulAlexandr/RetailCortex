## 1. EU rail freight market context: modal share, policy (Capacity Regulation, Greening Freight, TEN-T), single wagonload, cross-border, track works, state aid

### Takeaway
EU rail freight is shrinking, not growing. Tonne-km fell 1.8% in 2025, the fourth straight annual decline (~368bn tkm), and rail's share of land freight is stuck around 17%. Policy keeps adding demand for capacity-management software. The headline item is the new Rail Capacity Regulation (EU) 2026/1184, published 10 June 2026, whose first timetable applies in December 2030. Meanwhile incumbents are under heavy cost pressure: DB Cargo must survive without state support from 2026, and Germany's corridor blockades (Generalsanierung) are disrupting freight.

### Cited Findings
**Volumes and modal share**
- EU rail freight tonne-km fell **1.8% in 2025 vs 2024**. Germany is by far the largest contributor at **124bn tkm in 2025**, followed by Poland (**14.7%** of the EU total) and France (**9.3%**). Rail tonnage fell in 16 Member States and rose in 8. — [Eurostat, Railway freight transport statistics](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Railway_freight_transport_statistics)
- Trade press puts 2025 at **368.2bn tkm** and calls it the **fourth consecutive year of decline**, while road gains share. — [RailMarket](https://en.railmarket.com/news/insights/63118-eu-freight-rail-goes-against-its-target-four-years-of-decrease-while-road-gains-ground)
- Counting all modes including maritime, rail was **5.4%** of transport performed in 2024 and road **25.7%**. Since 2014 rail has lost 0.3 pp, while road was the only mode to gain share (+3.3 pp). — [Eurostat news, 26 Mar 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20260326-1)
- Counting only land modes, rail had about **17% of EU freight in 2024**, and "the shift to rail is not making progress". (Different denominators explain the 5.4% vs 17% gap. The commonly quoted ~17-18% figure is the land-modes share.) — [Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/Europa/EN/Topic/Transport/gueterverkehr-eisenbahn.html); [Eurostat modal split](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Freight_transport_statistics_-_modal_split)

**Rail Capacity Regulation (proposed July 2023 in the Greening Freight package): status in 2026 is ADOPTED**
- Trilogue political agreement in **Nov 2025**. The Parliament's TRAN committee approved it **35–5 on 27 Jan 2026**. Council adopted its first-reading position on **21 Apr 2026**. Parliament gave final approval on **19 May 2026**. It became **Regulation (EU) 2026/1184 of 20 May 2026**, published in the OJ on **10 June 2026**. — [EUR-Lex 2026/1184](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1184/oj); [Railway Gazette](https://www.railwaygazette.com/europe/2026/05/21/european-parliament-approves-regulation-on-railway-infrastructure-capacity/); [EU Perspectives](https://euperspectives.eu/2026/01/parliament-finalises-eu-cross-border-rail-capacity-deal-amid-push-for-network-efficiency/); [Council doc ST-16833-2025-REV-1](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-16833-2025-REV-1/en/pdf); [EU Law Live](https://eulawlive.com/regulation-on-the-use-of-railway-infrastructure-capacity-in-the-single-european-railway-area-published-in-oj/)
- Content:
  - Replaces the old capacity-allocation framework, partly overhauls Directive 2012/34/EU and **repeals Regulation 913/2010 on Rail Freight Corridors**.
  - Splits capacity planning into **three periods**: strategic (every 5 years), annual, and adaptation.
  - Sets **penalties of €1–€8 per train-km (up to €16 with a multiplier)** when infrastructure managers or railway undertakings miss capacity commitments.
  - **The first timetable under the new rules takes effect in December 2030.**
  - Sources: [EC DG MOVE news, 10 Jun 2026](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/new-eu-rules-will-improve-cross-border-rail-traffic-and-optimise-network-capacity-2026-06-10_en); [Global Railway Review (CER welcomes)](https://www.globalrailwayreview.com/cer-welcomes-new-eu-rail-capacity-regulation-approval/2135495.article); [CLECAT trilogue note](https://www.clecat.org/news/newsletters/trilogue-agreement-on-rail-capacity-regulation)
- The Council adopted its general approach on the capacity regulation in June 2024. — [Consilium press release, 18 Jun 2024](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/06/18/greening-freight-package-council-adopts-position-for-a-more-efficient-management-of-rail-infrastructure-capacity-and-traffic/)

### Inferences
- **Regulation-driven software demand with a hard deadline.** Regulation 2026/1184 requires infrastructure managers (IMs) to do 5-year strategic capacity planning, coordinate cross-border capacity and account for penalties at €1–16/train-km, all for the **Dec 2030 timetable**. Procurement and build windows are therefore roughly **2027–2030**. Likely needs:
  - capacity modelling and simulation;
  - planning and impact assessment for temporary capacity restrictions (TCRs);
  - cross-IM data exchange;
  - KPI and penalty reconciliation between IMs and railway undertakings (RUs).
  - Much of this will flow through RNE/IM tenders to incumbents (Siemens, Hitachi, IVU-type vendors, IM in-house IT). Analytics and optimisation modules are a more plausible startup wedge than the core system of record.
- **The German corridor programme is a live, observable pain point.** Diversion routes are overloaded and uncoordinated, plans keep being redrawn, and DB says diversion costs are "outside its financial remit". There is a concrete use case for **diversion and capacity simulation** and **RU-side replanning tools**: re-routing, rescheduling crews and locos, and ETA updates to customers.
- **Shrinking volumes and incumbent cost-cutting** (DB Cargo with no state aid from 2026, ~6,200 jobs) push operators toward software that cuts cost: yard and shunting planning, wagon and loco allocation, crew rostering, automated order intake. Willingness to pay will be tight, and sales cycles at state-owned incumbents are long. The faster buyers are likely private RUs and intermodal operators.
- CountEmissionsEU being voluntary and the Combined Transport revision being withdrawn weaken the "regulatory push" for shippers to choose rail. Do not build a business that depends on mandatory emissions reporting.

## 2. EU rail tech landscape: DAC, ERTMS/ETCS, TSI Telematics, capacity tools, wagon telematics, intermodal platforms, yard automation, predictive maintenance, digital twins

### Takeaway
The two flagship EU programmes are both stuck on money and lock-in, not on technology.
- **DAC** works technically but has **no deployment deadline and no EU budget line**. Fleet cost is €12–15bn, and the benefit-cost ratio is below 1 for the first 10 years.
- **ERTMS** covered only **~10% of TEN-T (12,400 km) at end-2024**. An Oct 2026 EU study says trackside rollout must speed up about 5x and documents supplier lock-in.

Software startups are better off in data, planning and analytics layers that sit beside these safety-certified systems than inside them.

### Cited Findings
**Digital Automatic Coupling (DAC)**
- Per-wagon cost estimates vary widely. EDDP's initial projections were **€5,000–17,000 per wagon**. Some CEE evaluations reach **€40–45k per wagon**, including digital systems and installation. An industry association cited €15–20k for a basic coupler. — [Railtarget](https://www.railtarget.eu/technologies-and-infrastructure/digital-automatic-coupling-europe-rail-freight-costs-benefits-dac-10781.html); [Rolling Stock World](https://rollingstockworld.com/components/fierce-debate-over-introduction-of-digital-automatic-couplers-in-europe/)
- A 2024 study commissioned by the German transport ministry (BMV) estimated **€12–15bn** initial investment to convert the European freight fleet. The **benefit-cost ratio stays below 1.0 in all scenarios for the first 10 years** and rises over 30 years. — [BMV DAC 2024 final report (PDF)](https://www.bmv.de/SharedDocs/EN/publications/dac-2024-final-report.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)
- A sector estimate cited in trade press is about **€13bn for ~450,000 wagons and ~17,000 locomotives**. Attribution within the search results was unclear; it appears in Railtarget/RollingStockWorld coverage. — [Railtarget](https://www.railtarget.eu/technologies-and-infrastructure/digital-automatic-coupling-europe-rail-freight-costs-benefits-dac-10781.html)
- **Financing idea:** the EUROFIMA CEO proposed hire-purchase at **€3.45 per wagon per day for 20 years**. — [RailFreight, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.railfreight.com/in-depth/2026/03/18/dac-installation-might-cost-less-than-4-euros-per-day/)

### Inferences
- **DAC:** do not build a company whose revenue depends on DAC rollout before about 2030. There is no EU funding line, no deadline, and the BCR is below 1 for 10 years. Two adjacent bets do not need DAC:
  - **Digital train-preparation data** (electronic consist/wagon lists, automated brake-test evidence, wagon condition data) that already works with today's telematics;
  - **DAC migration planning and financing analytics** for wagon keepers and lessors, which fits the EUROFIMA per-day leasing logic.
- **ERTMS** onboard and trackside (SIL4) is a closed oligopoly: Alstom, Siemens, Hitachi (ex-Thales), Wabtec, with CAF and Indra as smaller players. The Oct 2026 EU study about "repeat bills" and supplier lock-in points to a real gap in **engineering-data automation**: digital track and balise data preparation, test automation, virtual/lab testing, and vendor-neutral data models. These would reduce what IMs pay to re-engineer. Buyers are IMs and ministries, so sales are slow, but the political tailwind is real.
- **Telematics hardware is commoditising** (Nexxiot, Savvy, PJ Monitoring, Traxens, Railnova; most last raised money before 2024). The open opportunity is **vendor-neutral data products on top**: ETA prediction, wagon utilisation and turn-time analytics, condition-based maintenance for the entities in charge of maintenance (ECMs), and cross-keeper data pooling. Europe has no RailPulse-style neutral GPS standard (compare section 5).
- Big OEMs are buying inspection, detection and asset-data firms (Wabtec–Frauscher/Evident, Hitachi–Omnicom). That is a plausible **exit path for a predictive-maintenance or inspection-data startup**. It also signals that standalone "predictive maintenance platform" businesses struggle to scale; KONUX's funding seems to have stopped after 2021.

## 3. EU passenger rail tech: open access, ticketing and distribution, the Single Digital Booking & Ticketing Regulation, passenger rights, night trains, revenue management

### Takeaway
On **13 May 2026** the Commission proposed a rail ticketing regulation, **COM(2026) 232** (the "Single Digital Booking & Ticketing Regulation"), as part of a three-part passenger package. It would require operators to give ticket content and real-time data to third-party sellers and require dominant integrated platforms to sell rivals' tickets. It is still in the co-decision process (feedback closed 31 Jul 2026), so obligations probably start no earlier than about 2028–2029. Open-access competition is cutting fares (Spain), but entrants are financially fragile, especially after Spain's 2026 Adamuz disaster and the disruption that followed.

### Cited Findings
- **Passenger package, 13 May 2026:**
  - Three proposals: (1) a **rail ticketing regulation** so passengers can find, compare and buy multi-operator journeys as **one ticket in one transaction on a platform of their choice**; (2) a **multimodal digital mobility services / booking regulation**; (3) an **amendment to rail passenger rights**.
  - Mechanisms: operators must supply ticketing content and real-time data to online sellers, and dominant vertically integrated platforms must display and sell competitors' tickets.
  - Sources: [EC DG MOVE, 13 May 2026](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/one-journey-one-ticket-full-rights-commission-simplifies-europe-wide-travel-booking-and-train-travel-2026-05-13_en); [COM(2026) 232](https://aeur.eu/f/lwm); [Railway Gazette](https://www.railwaygazette.com/europe/2026/05/13/european-commission-unveils-passenger-package-to-tackle-fragmented-rail-booking-systems/); [EC proposal PDF](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a3284ec0-f1ad-4ee4-a558-5d4c17948046_en?filename=Proposal_on_rail_ticketing.pdf)
- The Commission feedback window ran **5 June–31 July 2026**. **CER** warned about dominant digital platforms and higher ticket prices; the **EPP group** welcomed the approach. — [Have Your Say](https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/have-your-say/initiatives/14626-EU-rules-on-multimodal-digital-mobility-services-and-single-digital-booking-ticketing); [IEU Monitoring](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/one-journey-one-ticket-eu-commission-pushes-single-market-for-cross-border-rail-travel/1216711)

- **Spain, open access under stress:**
  - **Ouigo and Iryo** asked for a **€119.3m/year cut** in Adif AV access-charge mark-ups, backed by a KPMG study, after demand fell.

  - Sources: [Railin](https://www.railin.net/en/news/flash/ouigo-and-iryo-call-for-a-e119-million-cut-in-high-speed-rail-access-charges/); [Wikipedia: 2026 Adamuz rail disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Adamuz_rail_disaster)

### Inferences
- **Regulation-driven B2B demand (2027–2030):** if COM(2026) 232 passes in roughly its current form, every rail operator, including small regional and open-access ones, will need:
  - **standardised distribution APIs** (likely OSDM-based);
  - real-time data feeds to resellers;
  - **through-ticket settlement and clearing between operators**;
  - **automated passenger-rights handling** (missed connections on combined tickets, compensation).
  - Incumbent distribution players (Siemens-owned Hacon/HAFAS, Amadeus, SilverRail, Trainline's B2B arm, Omio) will compete hard. Small operators and new entrants are a possible "compliance-in-a-box" segment.
- **Open-access entrants need lean revenue management (RM) and demand forecasting** but have small teams and fragile finances (Spain 2026). Disruption-aware forecasting, dynamic pricing under capacity shocks, and slot/path bidding analytics for new entrants are a coherent ML product. Risk: the customer base is small (perhaps tens of open-access operators EU-wide) and churns when entrants fail.
- Night trains and new entrants are visible but small. Treat them as niche customers, not a market.

RailState's "independent insights" positioning fits this. A data/ML founder could build shipper-side **service-performance evidence tooling** that serves merger conditions and also future switching petitions under a post-Part-1144 regime.
- **RailPulse making GPS the definitive demurrage record (Oct 2026) changes the economics.** Demurrage disputes become a data problem. The opening is **demurrage and accessorial audit/automation for shippers and railcar lessors**: reconciling railroad invoices against GPS, flagging billing errors, and predicting and avoiding constructive placement and storage. The buyer, the shipper, has budget and clear ROI.
- **ATI waivers move inspection from labour to data.** Volumes of geometry and imagery data grow, so **ML defect detection, triage and work-order integration** become valuable. Incumbent inspection players are consolidating (Loram, ENSCO, Wabtec/Evident, Hitachi/Omnicom), which suggests acquisition-driven exits. Political risk is real: unions oppose the waivers, and FRA's stance can shift.

## 7. Largest and least-served pain points; where incumbents lock things up and where software startups can win (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Incumbents (Siemens, Alstom, Hitachi incl. ex-Thales, Wabtec, Knorr-Bremse, Progress Rail) control **safety-certified, hardware-bound layers**: signalling/ERTMS/PTC, traffic management, onboard systems, and OEM maintenance platforms. The EU's own Oct 2026 study documents that lock-in. The underserved, software-addressable pains share four traits: **cross-organisation data**, **planning under disruption**, **shipper-side visibility and billing**, and **regulation-mandated data exchange**. These are areas where no single incumbent owns the data and new rules (Capacity Regulation, SDBTR, RailPulse GPS standard, ATI waivers, merger conditions) create deadlines.

### Cited Findings
- ERTMS supplier dependency, fewer bids and repeat engineering bills are raising costs. — [RailFreight, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.railfreight.com/railfreight/2026/10/28/repeat-bills-fewer-bids-and-supplier-lock-in-eu-study-maps-cost-of-ertms-fragmentation/)
- OEMs are buying detection, inspection and asset-data companies (Wabtec: Frauscher, Evident; Hitachi: Omnicom, Thales GTS). — [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-profiles/wabtec/); [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/ResearchInsight/rail-asset-management-market.asp)
- **Deadline-driven EU capacity management**: Regulation 2026/1184 applies to the **Dec 2030** timetable, with penalties of **€1–16/train-km**. — [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1184/oj); [EC](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/new-eu-rules-will-improve-cross-border-rail-traffic-and-optimise-network-capacity-2026-06-10_en)
- **Disruption from track works**: German freight diversions are overloaded and plans keep changing. — [RailFreight, Oct 2026](https://www.railfreight.com/railfreight/2026/10/09/germany-finalises-overhaul-of-dbs-troubled-corridor-renovation-plan-eight-delayed-projects-named/); [Railway Gazette](https://www.railwaygazette.com/germany/2026/06/19/hamburg-berlin-main-line-reopens-but-delays-fuel-backlash-over-dbs-corridor-blockade-model/)
- **Mandated ticket distribution data**: COM(2026) 232. — [EC](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/one-journey-one-ticket-full-rights-commission-simplifies-europe-wide-travel-booking-and-train-travel-2026-05-13_en)
- **Demurrage data standard**: RailPulse GPS becomes the definitive record from 1 Oct 2026. — [RailPulse](https://railpulse.com/)
- **ATI waiver**: 5 years from Dec 2025; CSX covers about 3,000 route miles. — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fra-greenlights-expanded-rail-track-tech-tests-as-csx-prepares-july-2026-rollout)
- **Merger-condition monitoring**: UP/NS customer assurances, with the STB decision due by Aug 2027. — [UP](https://www.up.com/press-releases/growth/stb-supplemental-second-round-270727); [Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/stb-lifts-abeyance-in-union-pacific-norfolk-southern-merger-and-sets-procedural-schedule/)

### Inferences
Ranked candidate white spaces for a data/ML/software founder. This ranking is the researcher's judgement, built from the cited findings above.

1. **EU: capacity and disruption planning (TCR/diversion simulation and replanning) for IMs and RUs.**
   - Pain: German Generalsanierung through 2036, the new Capacity Regulation with a 2030 deadline and penalties.
   - Hard optimisation and ML problems: timetable feasibility, conflict detection, delay propagation, re-routing with loco and crew constraints.
   - Incumbent overlap: IM in-house tools, RNE and big TMS vendors. The **RU-side planner (private freight operators)** is likely the most open wedge.
2. **US: shipper/lessor demurrage, accessorial and service-performance analytics** built on RailPulse GPS plus Railinc events.
   - Clear ROI, budget-holding buyers, and a new data standard from Oct 2026.
   - Extends into **merger-condition monitoring** and **switching-petition evidence**.
   - Risk: RailPulse or Telegraph might move into it themselves.
3. **EU: vendor-neutral wagon data layer** (ETA, utilisation, ECM condition-based maintenance) across telematics brands, a "RailPulse for Europe".
   - Telematics hardware exists (Nexxiot, Savvy, PJM, Traxens), but data is siloed per keeper and per vendor.
   - DAC delay means today's sensors stay the main data source.
4. **EU passenger: SDBTR/OSDM compliance-in-a-box plus RM for small and open-access operators.**
   - Timed to the regulation's likely adoption around 2027–2028.
   - Includes through-ticket settlement and passenger-rights automation.
   - Competes with Hacon (Siemens), Amadeus, SilverRail, Trainline B2B.
5. **Automated inspection ML (US ATI waivers; EU IMs)**: defect detection and triage from geometry, imagery and acoustic data, plus work-order integration.
   - Strong OEM acquisition interest (exit path).
   - Union and political risk in the US.
6. **ERTMS engineering-data automation** (track and balise data preparation, test automation) to cut "repeat bills".
   - Large spend (tens of €bn), but the buyers are conservative IMs and safety certification (CENELEC) is a barrier.
   - Best pursued with an integrator partner.

Areas a newcomer should probably avoid as a core product:
- onboard/trackside signalling (SIL4, oligopoly);
- DAC hardware;
- autonomous rolling stock (capex-heavy, already contested by Parallel, Intramotev, Voltify);
- generic "predictive maintenance platforms" (KONUX's funding plateau after 2021 and OEM bundling, e.g., Railigent/HealthHub, suggest a hard standalone market).

### Gaps
- No market-sizing data (TAM in € or $) was found for rail software segments beyond the Wabtec $8bn→$16bn inspection TAM claim. Generic market-research reports (MarketsandMarkets, Mordor) exist but are low quality.
- No customer surveys were retrieved quantifying shipper pain (e.g., car-order fulfilment rates, ETA accuracy, demurrage dispute volumes).
